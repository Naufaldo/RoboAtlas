# Sampling-Based Motion Planning: RRT & RRT*

> "In high-dimensional continuous spaces where grid discretization suffers from the curse of dimensionality, how can a robot quickly discover collision-free paths?"

Discrete graph search (like A*) requires discretizing continuous configuration spaces $\mathcal{C}_{\text{space}}$ into grids, which scales exponentially $\mathcal{O}(G^d)$ with dimension $d$. **Sampling-based motion planning** breaks this bottleneck by randomly sampling configurations in continuous space.

---

## 1. The Rapidly-exploring Random Tree (RRT)

RRT incrementally builds a space-filling tree $\mathcal{T} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ biased toward unexplored regions of free space $\mathcal{C}_{\text{free}}$:

1. **Sample Random State**: $q_{\text{rand}} \sim \mathcal{U}(\mathcal{C})$. (With goal bias probability $p_{\text{goal}} \approx 0.1$).
2. **Find Nearest Neighbor**:
   $$
   q_{\text{near}} = \arg\min_{v \in \mathcal{V}} \| v - q_{\text{rand}} \|
   $$
3. **Steer Step**: Extend toward $q_{\text{rand}}$ by incremental step size $\Delta q$:
   $$
   q_{\text{new}} = q_{\text{near}} + \min(\Delta q, \|q_{\text{rand}} - q_{\text{near}}\|) \cdot \frac{q_{\text{rand}} - q_{\text{near}}}{\|q_{\text{rand}} - q_{\text{near}}\|}
   $$
4. **Collision Check**: If the edge segment $(q_{\text{near}}, q_{\text{new}}) \subset \mathcal{C}_{\text{free}}$, add $q_{\text{new}}$ and directed edge $(q_{\text{near}}, q_{\text{new}})$ to $\mathcal{T}$.

---

## 2. Asymptotic Optimality in RRT*

Standard RRT is **probabilistically complete** (finds a path if one exists as $N \to \infty$), but its solution path is almost surely suboptimal.

**RRT*** (Karaman & Frazzoli, 2011) introduces two crucial optimizations within neighborhood radius $r_n = \gamma (\frac{\log n}{n})^{1/d}$:

### 1. Near Parent Selection
Instead of strictly connecting to $q_{\text{near}}$, RRT* searches all vertices $X_{\text{near}} \subset \mathcal{V}$ in radius $r_n$ and selects the parent $q_{\text{min}}$ that minimizes total accumulated path cost:

$$
q_{\text{min}} = \arg\min_{q \in X_{\text{near}}} \left( \text{Cost}(q) + \|q - q_{\text{new}}\| \right)
$$

### 2. Tree Rewiring
RRT* checks if routing existing neighbors $q_{\text{near}} \in X_{\text{near}}$ through the new vertex $q_{\text{new}}$ reduces their cost:

$$
\text{If } \text{Cost}(q_{\text{new}}) + \|q_{\text{new}} - q_{\text{near}}\| < \text{Cost}(q_{\text{near}}): \quad \text{Parent}(q_{\text{near}}) \leftarrow q_{\text{new}}
$$

As $N \to \infty$, the cost of the path found by RRT* converges almost surely to the theoretical optimum.
