# Mathematical Foundations & Geometry for Robotics

> "Why are linear algebra, skew-symmetric operators, multivariate calculus, and Gaussian probability theory the universal languages powering every autonomous robot from self-driving cars to bipedal humanoids?"

To navigate the physical world, articulate multi-link robotic manipulators, filter noisy sensor readings, or optimize large-scale SLAM pose graphs, a robot must translate continuous spatial geometry into precise numerical operations. This module provides the 5 core mathematical pillars supporting the entire RoboAtlas curriculum.

---

## 1. Vector Spaces & Spatial Distance Metrics ($\mathbb{R}^n$)

Physical obstacle points, target goal waypoints, and robot poses in 2D/3D space are represented as column vectors:

$$
\mathbf{p} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^3, \quad \mathbf{\hat{u}} = \frac{\mathbf{p}}{\|\mathbf{p}\|} \quad (\text{Unit Direction Vector})
$$

```text
┌───────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Distance Norm Metric      │ Mathematical Formula                     │ Typical Role in Autonomous Robotics                    │
├───────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ L2 Norm (Euclidean)       │ ||p||_2 = sqrt(x^2 + y^2 + z^2)          │ Straight-line LiDAR range, optical raycasting, cameras │
│ L1 Norm (Manhattan)       │ ||p||_1 = |x| + |y| + |z|                │ 4-connected grid A* graph search heuristic             │
│ L-Infinity (Chebyshev)    │ ||p||_inf = max(|x|, |y|, |z|)           │ 8-connected grid movement heuristic (diagonal moves)   │
│ Mahalanobis Distance      │ D_M = sqrt((p - mu)^T Sigma^-1 (p - mu)) │ Sensor data association validation gate in EKF/MCL     │
└───────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 2. Dot Product, Cross Product & Skew-Symmetric Matrices

1. **Dot Product (Inner Product)**:
   $$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{a}^T \mathbf{b} = \|\mathbf{a}\| \|\mathbf{b}\| \cos\theta$$
   Measures collinearity and yields orthogonal vector projection length $\mathbf{a}_{\parallel} = (\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{\hat{b}}) \mathbf{\hat{b}}$.

2. **Cross Product & Skew-Symmetric Operator $[\mathbf{v}]_\times$**:
   $$\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} = [\mathbf{a}]_\times \mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -a_z & a_y \\ a_z & 0 & -a_x \\ -a_y & a_x & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} b_x \\ b_y \\ b_z \end{bmatrix}$$
   Produces a vector mutually perpendicular to the plane spanned by $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$. In robotics, skew-symmetric matrices are Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ generators governing angular velocity rotation rates $\mathbf{\dot{R}} = [\boldsymbol{\omega}]_\times \mathbf{R}$.

## 3. Matrix Algebra, Special Orthogonal Group $SO(n)$, & SVD

Matrix operations map coordinates across coordinate frames:

1. **Matrix Multiplication & Transpose Inverses**: $(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B})^T = \mathbf{B}^T \mathbf{A}^T$ and $(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B})^{-1} = \mathbf{B}^{-1} \mathbf{A}^{-1}$.
2. **Special Orthogonal Rotation Groups $SO(2)$ & $SO(3)$**:
   $$\mathbf{R}^T \mathbf{R} = \mathbf{I}, \quad \det(\mathbf{R}) = +1$$
   *(Preserves Euclidean lengths and inter-vector angles rigidly without shear or dilation).*
3. **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)**:
   $$\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{V}^T$$
   Used universally in the *Iterative Closest Point (ICP)* algorithm for closed-form rotation recovery and in the *Moore-Penrose Pseudoinverse* $\mathbf{J}^+ = \mathbf{V} \mathbf{\Sigma}^+ \mathbf{U}^T$ for redundant robot arm inverse kinematics.

## 4. Eigenvalues, Eigenvectors & Covariance Uncertainty Ellipses

For a symmetric positive semi-definite covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma} \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}$:

$$
\mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{v}_i = \lambda_i \mathbf{v}_i
$$

- **Eigenvectors $\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2$**: Define the major and minor axis directions of the spatial uncertainty ellipse.
- **Eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$**: Determine the geometric semi-axis standard deviation lengths ($\sigma_1 = \sqrt{\lambda_1}, \sigma_2 = \sqrt{\lambda_2}$).

```text
                        2D COVARIANCE UNCERTAINTY ELLIPSE
                        
                                       ^ v_1 (Major Principal Direction)
                                      /
                                     /    .-----.
                                    /   .'       `.
                                   /   /           \
                                  +   |      + mu   |  <-- Semi-axis Length = sqrt(lambda_1)
                                 /     \           /
                                /       `.       .'
                               /          `-----'
                              /
                             /  v_2 (Minor Semi-axis, Length = sqrt(lambda_2))
```

## 5. Multivariate Calculus: Gradients, Jacobians, & Hessians

Most kinematic and sensor models in robotics are non-linear ($\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})$). Multivariate calculus linearizes systems via multivariate Taylor expansions:

$$
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}) \approx \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_0) + \mathbf{J}(\mathbf{x}_0)(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_0) + \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_0)^T \mathbf{H}(\mathbf{x}_0)(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_0)
$$

1. **Gradient Vector ($\nabla f$)**: Direction of steepest ascent, utilized in *Gradient Descent* and Artificial Potential Fields.
2. **Jacobian Matrix ($\mathbf{J} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{f}}{\partial \mathbf{x}}$)**: First-order partial derivative matrix mapping joint velocities to end-effector velocities ($\mathbf{\dot{x}} = \mathbf{J}\mathbf{\dot{q}}$) and linearizing EKF transition models ($\mathbf{F}_t, \mathbf{H}_t$).
3. **Hessian Matrix ($\mathbf{H} = \nabla^2 f$)**: Second-order curvature matrix utilized in non-linear least squares SLAM optimizers (*Gauss-Newton* and *Levenberg-Marquardt*).

## 6. Probability Theory & Linear Covariance Propagation

Because real sensors and actuators exhibit stochastic Gaussian noise, a robot's belief over its spatial state is modeled as a multivariate Gaussian random variable $\mathbf{x} \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \mathbf{\Sigma})$:

$$
p(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2} |\mathbf{\Sigma}|^{1/2}} \exp\left( -\frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})^T \mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}) \right)
$$

### Linear Covariance Propagation Law:
If a Gaussian state vector $\mathbf{x}$ with covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma}_x$ undergoes affine transformation $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}$, the output covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma}_y$ is:

$$
\mathbf{\Sigma}_y = \mathbf{A} \mathbf{\Sigma}_x \mathbf{A}^T
$$
