# Mathematical & Geometric Foundations for Robotics

> "Why is linear algebra, trigonometry, and coordinate geometry the universal language spoken by every autonomous robot?"

To navigate physical space, manipulate tools, or stabilize flight, every autonomous robot must represent continuous geometry through discrete numbers.

---

## 1. Physical Position Vectors & Cartesian Frames

In planar 2D robotics, the position of a robot landmark or sensor detection relative to origin $\{O\}$ is represented by a 2D Euclidean column vector:

$$
\mathbf{p} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^2
$$

### Vector Magnitude (Euclidean $L_2$ Distance):
By the Pythagorean theorem:

$$
\|\mathbf{p}\| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}
$$

### Unit Direction Vector:
$$
\mathbf{\hat{u}} = \frac{\mathbf{p}}{\|\mathbf{p}\|} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} \\ \frac{y}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta \\ \sin\theta \end{bmatrix}
$$

## 3. The Dot Product & Directional Alignment

When a robot tracks a path or computes sensor projections, it calculates the **inner dot product** between velocity vector $\mathbf{v}$ and target path vector $\mathbf{d}$:

$$
\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = a_x b_x + a_y b_y = \|\mathbf{a}\| \|\mathbf{b}\| \cos\theta
$$

### Geometric Interpretations:
1. **$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} > 0$** ($\theta < 90^\circ$): Moving towards target direction.
2. **$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0$** ($\theta = 90^\circ$): Strictly orthogonal / perpendicular.
3. **$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} < 0$** ($\theta > 90^\circ$): Moving away from target direction.
