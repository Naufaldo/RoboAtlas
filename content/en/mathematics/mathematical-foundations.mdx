# Mathematical & Geometric Foundations for Robotics

> "Why do linear algebra, multivariate calculus, and probability theory constitute the universal language powering every autonomous robot algorithm worldwide?"

To navigate continuous real-world environments, actuate multi-link articulated robot arms, filter sensor noise, or optimize non-linear SLAM factor graphs, a robot must mathematically map spatial geometry into precise numerical algorithms. This module introduces the four indispensable mathematical pillars powering the entire RoboAtlas curriculum.

---

## 1. Vector Spaces & Spatial Distance Metrics ($\mathbb{R}^n$)

The physical position of an obstacle, goal landmark, or robot end-effector is expressed as a column vector:

$$
\mathbf{p} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^3, \quad \mathbf{\hat{u}} = \frac{\mathbf{p}}{\|\mathbf{p}\|} \quad (\text{Unit Direction Vector})
$$

```text
┌───────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Metric Norm Definition    │ Mathematical Formula                     │ Robotics Application Role                              │
├───────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ L2 Euclidean Norm         │ ||p||_2 = sqrt(x^2 + y^2 + z^2)          │ Straight-line LiDAR range, camera raycasting           │
│ L1 Manhattan Norm         │ ||p||_1 = |x| + |y| + |z|                │ 4-connected grid A* graph search heuristic             │
│ L-Infinity Chebyshev Norm │ ||p||_inf = max(|x|, |y|, |z|)           │ 8-connected grid motion with uniform diagonal cost     │
│ Mahalanobis Distance      │ D_M = sqrt((p - mu)^T Sigma^-1 (p - mu)) │ Statistical outlier rejection in EKF/MCL data assoc.   │
└───────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 2. Matrix Algebra, The Special Orthogonal Group $SO(n)$, & SVD

Matrices perform spatial linear transformations across reference frames:

1. **Matrix Multiplication & Transposition**: $(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B})^T = \mathbf{B}^T \mathbf{A}^T$ and $(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B})^{-1} = \mathbf{B}^{-1} \mathbf{A}^{-1}$.
2. **Special Orthogonal Rotation Groups $SO(2)$ & $SO(3)$**:
   $$\mathbf{R}^T \mathbf{R} = \mathbf{I}, \quad \det(\mathbf{R}) = +1$$
   *(Preserves Euclidean lengths and mutual angular relationships rigidly without shear or volume distortion).*
3. **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)**:
   $$\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{V}^T$$
   Used fundamentally in closed-form *Iterative Closest Point (ICP)* rotation alignment and the *Moore-Penrose Pseudoinverse* $\mathbf{J}^+ = \mathbf{V} \mathbf{\Sigma}^+ \mathbf{U}^T$ for redundant robot arm inverse kinematics.

## 3. Multivariate Calculus: Gradients, Jacobians, & Hessians

Physical robot forward kinematics and sensor observation models are inherently non-linear ($\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})$). Multivariate calculus enables analytical linear approximation via Taylor series expansion:

$$
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}) \approx \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_0) + \mathbf{J}(\mathbf{x}_0)(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_0) + \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_0)^T \mathbf{H}(\mathbf{x}_0)(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_0)
$$

1. **Gradient Vector ($\nabla f$)**: The vector pointing in the direction of greatest rate of increase; powers Gradient Descent and Artificial Potential Field obstacle avoidance.
2. **Jacobian Matrix ($\mathbf{J} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{f}}{\partial \mathbf{x}}$)**: The first-order partial derivative matrix mapping joint velocities to end-effector velocities ($\mathbf{\dot{x}} = \mathbf{J}\mathbf{\dot{q}}$) and linearizing EKF motion/sensor models ($\mathbf{F}_t, \mathbf{H}_t$).
3. **Hessian Matrix ($\mathbf{H} = \nabla^2 f$)**: The second-order curvature matrix required by non-linear least squares optimizers in Pose Graph SLAM (*Gauss-Newton* and *Levenberg-Marquardt*).

## 4. Multivariate Probability & Covariance Propagation

Because physical actuators and sensors are subject to random noise, robot states are modeled as multivariate Gaussian random vectors $\mathbf{x} \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \mathbf{\Sigma})$:

$$
p(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2} |\mathbf{\Sigma}|^{1/2}} \exp\left( -\frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})^T \mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}) \right)
$$

### The Law of Linear Covariance Propagation:
If a random vector $\mathbf{x}$ with covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma}_x$ undergoes an affine linear transformation $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}$, its output covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma}_y$ is:

$$
\mathbf{\Sigma}_y = \mathbf{A} \mathbf{\Sigma}_x \mathbf{A}^T
$$

*(This fundamental law governs motion uncertainty expansion in Extended Kalman Filtering and FastSLAM).*
