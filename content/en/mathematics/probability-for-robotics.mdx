# Probability Theory & Bayesian Reasoning for Robotics (M10)

> "Robots never know the physical world with 100% certainty. How do we represent partial knowledge, update hypotheses, and reason under sensor noise?"

In classical deterministic programming, variables are binary (`true` or `false`). In autonomous robotics, variables are **random variables** governed by **probability distributions**.

---

## 1. Random Variables, Distributions & Expectations

Let $X$ be a random state variable (e.g. $X \in \{\text{Room A}, \text{Room B}\}$ or continuous coordinate $x \in \mathbb{R}$):

### 1.1 Probability Axioms
1. **Non-negativity**: $P(X = x) \ge 0 \quad \forall x$.
2. **Total Probability**: $\sum_x P(X = x) = 1$ (or $\int p(x) \, dx = 1$).

### 1.2 Mean (Expected Value) & Variance
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Expected Value } \mathbb{E}[X] &= \mu = \sum_x x \cdot P(X = x) \\
\text{Variance } \text{Var}(X) &= \sigma^2 = \mathbb{E}[(X - \mu)^2] = \sum_x (x - \mu)^2 P(X = x)
\end{aligned}
$$

---

## 2. Derivation: Conditional Probability & Bayes' Theorem

When a robot makes a noisy sensor observation $Z = z$, we want to calculate the updated belief $P(X \mid Z)$:

### 2.1 Conditional Probability
The probability of state $A$ given evidence $B$:

$$
P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} \quad (P(B) > 0)
$$

### 2.2 Law of Total Probability
$$
P(B) = \sum_i P(B \mid A_i) P(A_i)
$$

### 2.3 Bayes' Rule Formulation
Combining both gives the foundational formula of probabilistic robotics:

$$
P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(B \mid A) \cdot P(A)}{P(B)} = \frac{P(B \mid A) P(A)}{\sum_i P(B \mid A_i) P(A_i)}
$$

Where:
- **$P(A)$ (Prior Belief)**: What the robot believed *before* looking at the sensor.
- **$P(B \mid A)$ (Measurement Likelihood)**: How likely the sensor is to report $B$ if the robot is truly in state $A$.
- **$P(A \mid B)$ (Posterior Belief)**: The new updated confidence after fusing prior knowledge with sensor data.
