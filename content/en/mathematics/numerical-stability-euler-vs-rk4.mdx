# Numerical Stability & Integration: Euler vs Symplectic vs RK4 (M29)

> "Why do physics simulators catastrophically blow up when the integration timestep $\Delta t$ is slightly increased, and why are 4th-Order Runge-Kutta (RK4) and Symplectic integrators fundamental to robotics engineering?"

In robotics simulation and model predictive control, Newtonian equations of motion are formulated as systems of Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs):

$$
\mathbf{\dot{x}}(t) = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}(t), \mathbf{u}(t))
$$

Digital computers cannot evaluate continuous time analytically; instead, they step forward in discrete increments of $\Delta t = h$.

---

## 1. Dahlquist Test Equation & The $\mathcal{O}(h)$ Energy Blow-up

Consider the standard scalar linear test equation $\dot{x} = \lambda x$ with complex eigenvalue $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$ ($\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) < 0$ for stable physical systems):

```text
┌─────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Numerical Solver        │ Amplification Map R(z)   │ Stability Region |R(z)|  │ Max Timestep on Imaginary Axis         │
├─────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Explicit Euler       │ R(z) = 1 + z             │ Disk |1 + z| <= 1        │ 0 (Always unstable for pure oscillators│
│ 2. Symplectic Euler     │ Symplectic Area-Preserve │ Exact Phase Area Preserv │ h < 2 / omega_0 (Bounded oscillations) │
│ 3. Runge-Kutta 4 (RK4)  │ R(z) = 1 + z + ... + z^4 │ Broad left-half plane    │ h * omega_0 <= 2.828 (High stability)  │
└─────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

$$
\det(\mathbf{J}_{\text{Euler}}) = 1 + \omega_0^2 h^2 > 1
$$

Every Explicit Euler step injects artificial kinetic energy into conservative mechanical systems, causing phase trajectories to spiral outwards exponentially until floating-point overflow occurs.

---

## 2. Semi-Implicit Symplectic Euler (Hamiltonian Phase Volume Preservation)

In Hamiltonian classical mechanics, phase space volume $(q, p)$ is an exact physical invariant (Liouville's theorem). Symplectic Euler updates velocity first and evaluates the newly computed velocity for position integration:

$$
\mathbf{v}_{n+1} = \mathbf{v}_n + h \mathbf{a}(\mathbf{x}_n, \mathbf{v}_n)
$$

$$
\mathbf{x}_{n+1} = \mathbf{x}_n + h \mathbf{v}_{n+1}
$$

The transformation Jacobian determinant satisfies $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\text{Symplectic}}) = 1$. Total mechanical energy is rigorously bounded and oscillates stably around the true analytic value. This is why robotics physics engines (MuJoCo, Box2D, PhysX) rely on symplectic integrators as their default solvers!

---

## 3. Classical 4th-Order Runge-Kutta (RK4) & Butcher Tableau

RK4 samples four intermediate derivative slopes across the interval $[t_n, t_n + h]$:

$$
\mathbf{k}_1 = \mathbf{f}(t_n, \mathbf{y}_n)
$$

$$
\mathbf{k}_2 = \mathbf{f}\left(t_n + \frac{h}{2}, \mathbf{y}_n + \frac{h}{2}\mathbf{k}_1\right)
$$

$$
\mathbf{k}_3 = \mathbf{f}\left(t_n + \frac{h}{2}, \mathbf{y}_n + \frac{h}{2}\mathbf{k}_2\right)
$$

$$
\mathbf{k}_4 = \mathbf{f}(t_n + h, \mathbf{y}_n + h\mathbf{k}_3)
$$

### Simpson's Weighted State Update:

$$
\mathbf{y}_{n+1} = \mathbf{y}_n + \frac{h}{6} (\mathbf{k}_1 + 2\mathbf{k}_2 + 2\mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4)
$$

```text
BUTCHER TABLEAU FOR CLASSICAL RK4:
  0   │
 1/2  │ 1/2
 1/2  │  0   1/2
  1   │  0    0    1
──────┼───────────────────
      │ 1/6  1/3  1/3  1/6
```

With a local truncation error of $\mathcal{O}(h^5)$ and cumulative global error $\mathcal{O}(h^4)$, RK4 delivers exceptional numerical fidelity for smooth multi-body robot dynamics.

---

## 4. Mechanical Energy Conservation in a Pendulum

For a rigid pendulum of mass $m$ and length $l$:

$$
E(t) = \frac{1}{2} m l^2 \dot{\theta}^2 + m g l (1 - \cos\theta)
$$

- **Explicit Euler**: $\frac{dE}{dt} > 0$ (Energy drifts unbounded, causing the pendulum to gain speed endlessly).
- **Symplectic Euler**: $\langle \frac{dE}{dt} \rangle = 0$ (Energy bounded indefinitely).
- **RK4**: $\Delta E \sim \mathcal{O}(h^4)$ (Near-perfect energy preservation at practical step sizes).
