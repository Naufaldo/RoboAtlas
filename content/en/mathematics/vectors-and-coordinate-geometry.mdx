# Vectors, Coordinate Geometry & Euclidean Metrics (M01)

> "In physical space, how do we represent where a robot is located and in what direction its velocity or actuators are pushing?"

Robotics operates in Euclidean physical space. To command robot motion, plan trajectories, and interpret sensor rays, we must describe quantities that have both **magnitude** (how much) and **direction** (which way).

---

## 1. Scalars vs. Vectors

1. **Scalar**: A single real numerical quantity describing magnitude only (e.g., robot mass $m = 12.5\text{ kg}$, battery temperature $T = 38.2^\circ\text{C}$, time $t = 4.2\text{ s}$).
2. **Vector**: An ordered list of numbers representing a directed geometric arrow in space.

In 2D planar space, a position or velocity vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^2$ is written in Cartesian component form:

$$
\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} v_x \\ v_y \end{bmatrix}
$$

---

## 2. Derivation: Magnitude, Normalization & Polar Form

### 2.1 Euclidean Magnitude (Norm)
By the Pythagorean Theorem, the length of vector $\mathbf{v}$ (its Euclidean $L_2$ norm) is:

$$
\|\mathbf{v}\| = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2}
$$

### 2.2 Unit Direction Vector (Normalization)
To isolate pure spatial direction with unit length $\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}\| = 1$:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{v}\|} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{v_x}{\sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2}} \\ \frac{v_y}{\sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2}} \end{bmatrix}
$$

### 2.3 Cartesian to Polar Conversion
A 2D vector can be equivalently expressed by its length $r = \|\mathbf{v}\|$ and counter-clockwise angle $\theta$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2} \\
\theta &= \text{atan2}(v_y, v_x) \\
v_x &= r \cos\theta, \qquad v_y = r \sin\theta
\end{aligned}
$$

*(We use `atan2(y, x)` instead of $\arctan(y/x)$ to unambiguously determine the correct quadrant from $-\pi$ to $+\pi$).*
