# Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) Non-Linear 2D Localization

> "How can classical linear Kalman filtering be applied to mobile robots where wheel kinematics involve trigonometric functions and range-bearing sensors involve non-linear Euclidean distances and arctangents?"

In mobile robotics, both the kinematic forward motion model (containing trigonometric sines and cosines of heading $\theta$) and the exteroceptive sensor observation model (containing non-linear square roots and $\text{atan2}$ angles) are intrinsically **non-linear**. The **Extended Kalman Filter (EKF)** resolves this non-linearity by executing a **first-order Taylor series expansion** around the current state estimate, utilizing multivariate partial derivative matrices (**Jacobian Matrices $\mathbf{F}_t$ and $\mathbf{H}_t$**).

## 1. State Vector & Non-Linear Kinematic Motion Model

The robot state is defined in the $SE(2)$ plane: $\mathbf{x} = [x, y, \theta]^T$.

### Step 1: State & Covariance Prediction (Motion Model)
Given commanded linear velocity $v_t$ and angular velocity $\omega_t$:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t^- = f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}, \mathbf{u}_t) = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1} + \begin{bmatrix} v_t \Delta t \cos(\hat{\theta}_{t-1}) \\ v_t \Delta t \sin(\hat{\theta}_{t-1}) \\ \omega_t \Delta t \end{bmatrix}
$$

The motion model Jacobian matrix $\mathbf{F}_t = \left. \frac{\partial f}{\partial \mathbf{x}} \right|_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}}$:

$$
\mathbf{F}_t = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -v_t \Delta t \sin(\hat{\theta}_{t-1}) \\ 0 & 1 & v_t \Delta t \cos(\hat{\theta}_{t-1}) \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

A priori covariance prediction incorporating process noise $\mathbf{Q}_t$:

$$
\mathbf{\Sigma}_t^- = \mathbf{F}_t \mathbf{\Sigma}_{t-1} \mathbf{F}_t^T + \mathbf{Q}_t
$$

*(The confidence uncertainty ellipse expands along the direction of motion).*

---

## 2. Landmark Observation Measurement Update

When the sensor detects landmark $j$ located at known map coordinates $(m_{jx}, m_{jy})$ with range-bearing observation $\mathbf{z}_t = [r_t, \phi_t]^T$:

$$
h(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t^-, \mathbf{m}_j) = \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{(m_{jx} - \hat{x}_t^-)^2 + (m_{jy} - \hat{y}_t^-)^2} \\ \text{atan2}(m_{jy} - \hat{y}_t^-, m_{jx} - \hat{x}_t^-) - \hat{\theta}_t^- \end{bmatrix}
$$

The measurement Jacobian matrix $\mathbf{H}_t = \left. \frac{\partial h}{\partial \mathbf{x}} \right|_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t^-}$:

$$
\mathbf{H}_t = \begin{bmatrix} -\frac{m_{jx} - \hat{x}}{q} & -\frac{m_{jy} - \hat{y}}{q} & 0 \\ \frac{m_{jy} - \hat{y}}{q^2} & -\frac{m_{jx} - \hat{x}}{q^2} & -1 \end{bmatrix} \quad \left(q = \sqrt{(m_{jx} - \hat{x})^2 + (m_{jy} - \hat{y})^2}\right)
$$

### The Full Correction Cycle:
1. **Innovation Residual**: $\mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{z}_t - h(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t^-, \mathbf{m}_j)$
2. **Innovation Covariance**: $\mathbf{S}_t = \mathbf{H}_t \mathbf{\Sigma}_t^- \mathbf{H}_t^T + \mathbf{R}_t$
3. **Optimal Kalman Gain**: $\mathbf{K}_t = \mathbf{\Sigma}_t^- \mathbf{H}_t^T \mathbf{S}_t^{-1}$
4. **Posterior State Estimate**: $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_t^- + \mathbf{K}_t \mathbf{y}_t$
5. **Posterior Covariance Update**: $\mathbf{\Sigma}_t = (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_t \mathbf{H}_t) \mathbf{\Sigma}_t^-$

*(The $3\sigma$ confidence uncertainty ellipse sharply contracts around the true robot pose).*
