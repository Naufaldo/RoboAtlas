# Mobile Robot Localization Fundamentals & Prerequisites

> "If a mobile robot relies purely on wheel rotation counters, its position estimate is guaranteed to diverge within seconds. Why is map-based localization indispensable?"

**Robot Localization** is the fundamental problem of estimating a robot's pose $\mathbf{x} = [x, y, \theta]^T$ relative to a known global environmental map. Without continuous, consistent localization, collision-free autonomous path planning and goal navigation are mathematically impossible.

![Mobile Robot Localization Fundamentals](/images/sensors/localization-fundamentals.jpg)

---

## 1. Why Dead-Reckoning (Odometry Alone) Inevitably Fails

When a vehicle integrates only internal proprioceptive sensor measurements (such as optical wheel encoders or IMUs):
- Minor wheel radius errors or micro-slippage $\delta s$ accumulate monotonically over time.
- Heading errors $\Delta \theta$ act as an angular lever arm, amplifying lateral position errors linearly with travel distance ($e_{\text{lat}} \propto d \cdot \sin \Delta \theta$).
- **The probability distribution of robot pose expands indefinitely**, forming a diverging uncertainty drift cone.

**The Solution**: Use exteroceptive sensors (LiDAR, Cameras, Sonar) to match observed environmental landmark features $z_t$ against a known prior map (*Map Matching & Data Association*).

---

## 2. Taxonomy of Localization Problems

1. **Position Tracking (*Local Localization*)**:
   - The initial robot pose $\mathbf{x}_0$ is known with high certainty.
   - Objective: Compensate for incremental motion noise as the robot drives along its path.
2. **Global Localization**:
   - The initial robot pose is completely unknown (*uniform prior distribution* across entire map).
   - Objective: Estimate robot pose from scratch using initial sensor scans.
3. **The Kidnapped Robot Problem**:
   - A robot operating normally is teleported or displaced to another location without sensor notification.
   - The most rigorous benchmark for probabilistic state estimators to recover from a confident but incorrect belief (*multi-modal recovery*).
