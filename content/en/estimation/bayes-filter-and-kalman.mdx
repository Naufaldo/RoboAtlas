# Recursive Bayesian Filtering & 1D Kalman Filter State Estimation

> "When both robot actuation and sensor observations contain inherent physical noise, how do we recursively estimate the robot's true state?"

The fundamental paradigm of probabilistic robotics is the **Recursive Bayesian Filter**. It maintains a probability distribution $\text{bel}(x_t) = p(x_t \mid z_{1:t}, u_{1:t})$ representing the robot's belief over its true continuous state $x_t$.

## 1. The Universal Two-Step Predict-Correct Cycle

At every discrete control cycle $t$, the Bayesian filter executes two fundamental probabilistic updates:

```text
Belief bel(x_{t-1})  ──[1. Motion Prediction]──>  Prior bel_bar(x_t)  ──[2. Sensor Measurement]──>  Posterior bel(x_t)
```

### Step 1: Motion Prediction (Chapman-Kolmogorov Equation)
When a control action $u_t$ is applied, the belief distribution propagates forward via the robot's motion model $p(x_t \mid u_t, x_{t-1})$:

$$
\bar{\text{bel}}(x_t) = \int p(x_t \mid u_t, x_{t-1}) \, \text{bel}(x_{t-1}) \, dx_{t-1}
$$

*(Because motor motion adds mechanical uncertainty $Q$, the belief curve flattens and widens).*

### Step 2: Measurement Correction (Bayes' Rule)
When a sensor measurement $z_t$ is observed with likelihood $p(z_t \mid x_t)$:

$$
\text{bel}(x_t) = \eta \cdot p(z_t \mid x_t) \cdot \bar{\text{bel}}(x_t)
$$

*(Multiplying the predicted prior by sensor likelihood sharpens the distribution, driving variance down).*

## 2. Closed-Form 1D Kalman Filter Equations

Under linear dynamics and Gaussian noise distributions $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, the continuous integral simplifies into analytical closed-form linear algebra:

1. **Prediction**:
   $$
   \bar{\mu}_t = \mu_{t-1} + u_t, \qquad \bar{\sigma}^2_t = \sigma^2_{t-1} + Q
   $$
2. **Kalman Gain $K_t$**:
   $$
   K_t = \frac{\bar{\sigma}^2_t}{\bar{\sigma}^2_t + R}
   $$
3. **Measurement Correction**:
   $$
   \mu_t = \bar{\mu}_t + K_t (z_t - \bar{\mu}_t), \qquad \sigma^2_t = (1 - K_t) \bar{\sigma}^2_t
   $$

Where $Q$ is motion noise variance and $R$ is sensor noise variance.
