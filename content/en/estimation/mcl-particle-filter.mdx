# Monte Carlo Localization (MCL) Particle Filters

> "When wheel slippage and sensor noise corrupt measurements, how can a robot determine its true location with pinpoint certainty?"

In real-world robotics, determinism is an illusion. Odometry drifts, and sensor readings contain Gaussian noise. **Monte Carlo Localization (MCL)** solves the global localization problem by representing probability distributions over robot poses $\mathbf{x} = [x, y, \theta]^T$ using a cloud of weighted particles.

![Monte Carlo Localization (MCL) Particle Filter Cycle](/images/sensors/mcl-particle-filter-cycle.jpg)

---

## 1. The Recursive Bayesian Filtering Cycle

MCL implements a recursive Bayes filter over continuous state space:

$$
p(\mathbf{x}_t \mid z_{1:t}, u_{1:t}) = \eta \cdot p(z_t \mid \mathbf{x}_t) \int p(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_{t-1}, u_t) p(\mathbf{x}_{t-1} \mid z_{1:t-1}, u_{1:t-1}) \, d\mathbf{x}_{t-1}
$$

In a Particle Filter, the posterior belief is approximated by a set of $M$ particles:

$$
\mathcal{X}_t = \{ \langle \mathbf{x}_t^{[m]}, w_t^{[m]} \rangle \}_{m=1}^M
$$

---

## 2. The 3 Core Algorithmic Steps

### Step 1: Motion Prediction (Propagate Particles)
Sample each particle forward using the kinematic motion model corrupted by noise $\epsilon$:

$$
\mathbf{x}_t^{[m]} \sim p(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_{t-1}^{[m]}, u_t)
$$

### Step 2: Measurement Correction (Weight Calculation)
Compute importance weights by evaluating how likely the observed sensor beacon distances $z_t$ match expected distances $\hat{z}$:

$$
w_t^{[m]} = \prod_{k=1}^K \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} \exp\left( -\frac{(z_t^k - \hat{z}_t^{k,[m]})^2}{2\sigma^2} \right)
$$

### Step 3: Low-Variance Resampling
Draw $M$ new particles with probability proportional to weights $w_t^{[m]}$. Particles with high likelihood survive and duplicate; particles with poor likelihood perish.
