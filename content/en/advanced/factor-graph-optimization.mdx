# Factor Graph Optimization & Non-Linear Least Squares for SLAM

> "Why did classical EKF-SLAM suffer from quadratic $O(N^2)$ computational complexity and irreversible early linearization errors, while modern Factor Graph smoothing optimizes thousands of robot poses in milliseconds?"

In modern robotics (GTSAM, g2o, Ceres Solver, and Cartographer), full Simultaneous Localization and Mapping is formulated as a bipartite **Factor Graph**, modeling the Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) joint state distribution via Non-Linear Least Squares optimization.

---

## 1. Factor Graph Topology & Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) Formulation

A factor graph comprises two node types:
1. **Variable Nodes ($\Theta = \{ \mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_N, \mathbf{l}_1, \dots, \mathbf{l}_M \}$)**: Unknown robot poses and spatial landmark positions.
2. **Factor Nodes ($\phi_k(\Theta_k)$)**: Probabilistic sensor constraints (priors, odometry relative links, and landmark observations).

$$
P(\Theta \mid Z) \propto \prod_{k} \phi_k(\Theta_k) = \prod_{k} \exp\left( -\frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{e}_k(\Theta_k)\|_{\mathbf{\Sigma}_k}^2 \right)
$$

### Non-Linear Least Squares Objective:

$$
\Theta^* = \arg\min_{\Theta} \sum_{k} \mathbf{e}_k(\Theta_k)^T \mathbf{\Omega}_k \mathbf{e}_k(\Theta_k), \quad \mathbf{\Omega}_k = \mathbf{\Sigma}_k^{-1} \, (\text{Information Matrix})
$$

---

## 2. Gauss-Newton Iteration & Sparse Normal Equations

At each iteration, residual error vectors are linearized using Jacobians $\mathbf{J}_k = \frac{\partial \mathbf{e}_k}{\partial \Theta}$:

$$
\mathbf{H} \Delta \Theta = -\mathbf{b}
$$

Where:

$$
\mathbf{H} = \sum_{k} \mathbf{J}_k^T \mathbf{\Omega}_k \mathbf{J}_k \quad (\text{Sparse Information Matrix / System Hessian})
$$

$$
\mathbf{b} = \sum_{k} \mathbf{J}_k^T \mathbf{\Omega}_k \mathbf{e}_k
$$

### Manifold Retraction Step:

$$
\Theta \leftarrow \Theta \boxplus \Delta \Theta
$$

Because robots only observe local landmarks and temporally adjacent poses, the information matrix $\mathbf{H}$ is **strictly sparse**, enabling linear-time $O(N)$ solution via incremental variable elimination (*iSAM2 Bayes tree*).
