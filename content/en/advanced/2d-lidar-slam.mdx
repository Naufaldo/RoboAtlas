# 2D LiDAR SLAM Paradigms: Gmapping, Hector & Google Cartographer

> "How did 2D mobile SLAM evolve from monolithic particle-grid filters (Gmapping) into hierarchical submap-based pose graph optimization with branch-and-bound scan matching (Google Cartographer)?"

In the domain of autonomous ground vehicles (AGVs, warehouse mobile robots, robotic vacuum cleaners), **2D planar LiDAR SLAM** represents the gold-standard baseline for real-time indoor navigation. Based on the comprehensive review (*MDPI 2025*), 2D LiDAR SLAM paradigms are organized across three distinct algorithmic generations:

## 2. Google Cartographer Architectural Breakdown

Google Cartographer solves the scalability bottleneck of pose-graph SLAM via a two-tier hierarchical framework:

### Tier 1: Local SLAM & Submaps
- Time-synchronized, motion-undistorted laser scans are registered into an **active local submap** using *Real-Time Correlative Scan Matching (CSM)* followed by Ceres non-linear gradient optimization.
- Because each submap aggregates only a small window of scans, local construction is computationally cheap and drift-free.

### Tier 2: Global SLAM & Loop Closure Optimization
- Once a submap is finalized (*frozen*), a multi-resolution **Branch-and-Bound (B&B)** search identifies scan-to-submap matches across all historical submaps.
- When valid loop closure constraint edges are discovered, a sparse **Levenberg-Marquardt / Ceres Solver** globally relaxes all pose nodes and submap origins to eliminate accumulated odometry drift.
