# Serial Manipulator Kinematics & Analytical Inverse Kinematics

> "Given a desired tool position in physical 3D space, how do we solve the exact motor joint angles needed to reach that point?"

Articulated robotic arms (such as 6-DOF industrial welding robots or surgical manipulators) consist of a kinematic chain of rigid links connected by motorized revolute or prismatic joints.

---

## 1. Forward Kinematics (FK)

Forward kinematics maps known joint angles $(\theta_1, \theta_2)$ to the resulting end-effector Cartesian coordinate $(x, y)$:

For a planar 2-link robotic arm with link lengths $l_1$ and $l_2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= l_1 \cos\theta_1 + l_2 \cos(\theta_1 + \theta_2) \\
y &= l_1 \sin\theta_1 + l_2 \sin(\theta_1 + \theta_2)
\end{aligned}
$$

Forward kinematics always yields a unique, deterministic solution.

---

## 2. Analytical Inverse Kinematics (IK)

Inverse kinematics solves the reverse problem: given desired target coordinates $(x, y)$, determine the required joint angles $(\theta_1, \theta_2)$.

### Step 1: Law of Cosines for Elbow Joint $\theta_2$
Applying the law of cosines to the triangle formed by origin, elbow, and end-effector:

$$
D = \frac{x^2 + y^2 - l_1^2 - l_2^2}{2 l_1 l_2}
$$

- If $|D| > 1$: Target is outside the reachable workspace circle ($r > l_1 + l_2$).
- If $|D| \le 1$: Two symmetrical kinematic solutions exist (**Elbow-Up** vs. **Elbow-Down**):

$$
\theta_2 = \text{atan2}\left( \pm\sqrt{1 - D^2}, D \right)
$$

### Step 2: Shoulder Joint $\theta_1$
$$
\theta_1 = \text{atan2}(y, x) - \text{atan2}\left( l_2 \sin\theta_2, \, l_1 + l_2 \cos\theta_2 \right)
$$
