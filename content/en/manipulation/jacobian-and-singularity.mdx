# Manipulator Jacobian, Velocity Ellipsoids & Kinematic Singularities (M20)

> "What mathematical tool translates instantaneous motor rotational speeds into smooth tool tip velocities, and why does an outstretched arm lose the ability to move radially outward?"

The **Manipulator Jacobian Matrix $J(\mathbf{q})$** is the differential bridge linking joint velocity space $\dot{\mathbf{q}}$ to operational Cartesian velocity space $\dot{\mathbf{x}}$.

---

## 1. Mathematical Derivation of the 2-DOF Planar Jacobian

From forward kinematics:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= L_1 \cos(q_1) + L_2 \cos(q_1 + q_2) \\
y &= L_1 \sin(q_1) + L_2 \sin(q_1 + q_2)
\end{aligned}
$$

Taking the time derivatives using the chain rule ($\dot{x} = \frac{\partial x}{\partial q_1}\dot{q}_1 + \frac{\partial x}{\partial q_2}\dot{q}_2$):

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{x} \\ \dot{y} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -L_1 \sin q_1 - L_2 \sin(q_1+q_2) & -L_2 \sin(q_1+q_2) \\ L_1 \cos q_1 + L_2 \cos(q_1+q_2) & L_2 \cos(q_1+q_2) \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \dot{q}_1 \\ \dot{q}_2 \end{bmatrix}
$$

In compact matrix form:

$$
\dot{\mathbf{x}} = J(\mathbf{q}) \dot{\mathbf{q}}
$$

---

## 2. Jacobian Determinant & Singularity Conditions

The determinant of the $2 \times 2$ Jacobian matrix is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\det(J) &= J_{11} J_{22} - J_{12} J_{21} \\
&= (-L_1 s_1 - L_2 s_{12})(L_2 c_{12}) - (-L_2 s_{12})(L_1 c_1 + L_2 c_{12}) \\
&= -L_1 L_2 (s_1 c_{12} - c_1 s_{12}) \\
&= L_1 L_2 \sin(q_2)
\end{aligned}
$$

### Kinematic Singularities
A singularity occurs whenever $\det(J) = 0$, which happens when:

$$
\sin(q_2) = 0 \implies q_2 = 0^\circ \quad \text{or} \quad q_2 = 180^\circ
$$

1. **Boundary Singularity ($q_2 = 0^\circ$)**: The arm is fully outstretched ($r = L_1 + L_2$). No joint combination can produce an instantaneous velocity in the radial direction outward.
2. **Interior Singularity ($q_2 = 180^\circ$)**: The forearm folds back directly onto the upper arm ($r = |L_1 - L_2|$).

---

## 3. Manipulability Ellipsoid (Velocity Ellipse)

Mapping a unit hypersphere of joint velocities $\|\dot{\mathbf{q}}\|^2 \le 1$ into Cartesian velocity space produces a **Velocity Ellipse**:

$$
\dot{\mathbf{x}}^T (J J^T)^{-1} \dot{\mathbf{x}} \le 1
$$

- When the arm is well-conditioned ($\sin q_2 \approx 1$), the ellipse is round and isotropic.
- As the robot approaches a singularity ($\det(J) \to 0$), the ellipse collapses into a 1D line segment. Yoshikawa's manipulability index $w = \sqrt{\det(J J^T)} = |\det(J)| \to 0$.
