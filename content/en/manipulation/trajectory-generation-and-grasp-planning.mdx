# Trajectory Generation, Polynomial Splines & Robotic Grasp Planning

> "How does an industrial robot manipulator move smoothly between pick-and-place waypoints without jerking the motors, and what mathematical conditions guarantee that a two-fingered gripper firmly grasps an unknown object without dropping or crushing it?"

Executing physical manipulation tasks requires two coordinated capabilities: **Trajectory Generation** (computing continuous, smooth time-parameterized paths $\mathbf{q}(t)$ that respect motor velocity, acceleration, and jerk limits) and **Grasp Planning** (synthesizing contact forces that resist arbitrary external disturbance wrenches).

## 1. Joint Space Polynomial Trajectories

A path describes the purely geometric curve $\mathbf{q}(s)$, whereas a **trajectory** specifies the time-dependent motion $\mathbf{q}(t)$ specifying position, velocity $\dot{\mathbf{q}}(t)$, and acceleration $\ddot{\mathbf{q}}(t)$ for $t \in [0, T]$.

### 1. Cubic Polynomial Splines (Degree 3):
Given boundary conditions: start position $q_0$, final position $q_f$, start velocity $\dot{q}_0 = 0$, and final velocity $\dot{q}_f = 0$ over duration $T$:

$$
q(t) = a_0 + a_1 t + a_2 t^2 + a_3 t^3
$$

Solving for coefficients yields:
$$
a_0 = q_0, \quad a_1 = 0, \quad a_2 = \frac{3(q_f - q_0)}{T^2}, \quad a_3 = -\frac{2(q_f - q_0)}{T^3}
$$

### 2. Quintic Polynomial Splines (Degree 5) for Zero Jerk:
When starting and ending accelerations must also be strictly zero ($\ddot{q}_0 = 0, \ddot{q}_f = 0$):

$$
q(t) = q_0 + (q_f - q_0) \left[ 10\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)^3 - 15\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)^4 + 6\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)^5 \right]
$$

## 2. Trapezoidal & S-Curve Velocity Profiles

In high-speed manufacturing, motors operate best at constant maximum velocity $v_{\text{max}}$ with bounded acceleration $a_{\text{max}}$. A **Trapezoidal Profile** divides the trajectory into 3 phases:
1. **Acceleration phase**: $\dot{q}(t) = a_{\text{max}} t$
2. **Cruising phase**: $\dot{q}(t) = v_{\text{max}} = \text{const}$
3. **Deceleration phase**: $\dot{q}(t) = v_{\text{max}} - a_{\text{max}} (t - T + t_a)$

## 3. Robotic Grasp Mechanics: The Grasp Matrix $G$

When a multi-fingered hand or parallel-jaw gripper contacts an object at $n$ contact points $\{\mathbf{p}_1, \dots, \mathbf{p}_n\}$ with contact force vectors $\mathbf{f}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$:

The total wrench $\mathbf{w}_{\text{ext}} = [\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}}, \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{net}}]^T \in \mathbb{R}^6$ transmitted to the object center of mass is governed by the **Grasp Matrix $G \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 3n}$**:

$$
\mathbf{w}_{\text{ext}} = G \, \mathbf{f} = \begin{bmatrix}
I_{3\times 3} & \cdots & I_{3\times 3} \\
[\mathbf{p}_1]_\times & \cdots & [\mathbf{p}_n]_\times
\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{f}_1 \\ \vdots \\ \mathbf{f}_n \end{bmatrix}
$$

### Contact Friction Cones (Coulomb Friction):
At each contact point with surface normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_i$ and friction coefficient $\mu$:
$$
\|\mathbf{f}_{i,\text{tangential}}\| \le \mu f_{i,\text{normal}}
$$

### Force Closure vs. Form Closure:
- **Form Closure**: The object is completely immobilized geometrically by contact points alone without needing friction.
- **Force Closure**: The gripper contact forces can resist **any arbitrary external disturbance wrench $\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^6$** while all contact forces remain inside their respective friction cones:
  $$\text{cone}(G(\mathcal{F})) = \mathbb{R}^6$$

---

## 4. Antipodal Grasps for Parallel-Jaw Grippers

For a standard parallel-jaw gripper contacting an object at two points $\mathbf{p}_1$ and $\mathbf{p}_2$, an **antipodal grasp** is achieved if and only if the line connecting $\mathbf{p}_1$ and $\mathbf{p}_2$ lies strictly inside **both** friction cones:

$$
\theta_1 \le \arctan(\mu), \quad \theta_2 \le \arctan(\mu)
$$
