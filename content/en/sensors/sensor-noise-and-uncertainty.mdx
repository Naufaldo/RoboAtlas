# Sensor Noise Models, Gaussian Uncertainty & Probability Density (M08)

> "No physical sensor is ever 100% perfect. How do we model measurement noise mathematically so our robot can distinguish true state from sensor corruption?"

In real-world robotics, sensors (LiDAR, ultrasonic, optical encoders, IMU, GPS) are corrupted by physical thermal fluctuations, quantization errors, and environmental interference.

---

## 1. The Additive Measurement Model

The relationship between ground-truth state $x$ and actual sensor reading $z$ is:

$$
z = h(x) + b + \epsilon
$$

Where:
- $h(x)$ is the ideal physical measurement function.
- $b$ is the constant or slow-drifting **systematic bias** (e.g., zero-offset error).
- $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ is the zero-mean random **Gaussian noise** with variance $\sigma^2$.

---

## 2. Derivation: The Gaussian Probability Density Function (PDF)

By the **Central Limit Theorem**, compounding microscopic physical noise sources aggregate into a normal (Gaussian) distribution:

$$
p(z \mid x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} \exp\left( -\frac{(z - h(x) - b)^2}{2\sigma^2} \right)
$$

### Sample Statistics (Empirical Estimation)
Given $N$ recorded sensor samples $z_1, z_2, \dots, z_N$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Sample Mean } \bar{z} &= \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N z_i \\
\text{Sample Variance } s^2 &= \frac{1}{N-1} \sum_{i=1}^N (z_i - \bar{z})^2
\end{aligned}
$$

### Confidence Intervals (Empirical Rule):
- **$1\sigma$ Confidence**: $68.27\%$ of readings lie within $[\mu - \sigma, \mu + \sigma]$.
- **$2\sigma$ Confidence**: $95.45\%$ of readings lie within $[\mu - 2\sigma, \mu + 2\sigma]$.
- **$3\sigma$ Confidence**: $99.73\%$ of readings lie within $[\mu - 3\sigma, \mu + 3\sigma]$.
