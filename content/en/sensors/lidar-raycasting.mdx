# LiDAR Raycasting, Beam Intersection & Point Cloud Extraction (M21)

> "How does a spinning laser sensor convert optical Time-of-Flight (ToF) reflections into thousands of 2D/3D spatial obstacle points for autonomous mapping and SLAM?"

**Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR)** is the primary exteroceptive sensor for mobile robotics. A 2D LiDAR spins rapidly, projecting hundreds of pulsed laser beams to measure distances to surrounding obstacle boundaries.

![LiDAR Measurement Principle: Time-of-Flight (ToF), 360° Scanning Beams, and 2D/3D Spatial Point Cloud Extraction](/images/sensors/lidar-working-principle.jpg)

## 1. Raycasting Geometry & Parametric Intersection

Let the robot/sensor center be at global coordinate $\mathbf{p}_R = [x_R, y_R]^T$ with heading $\theta_R$.

For the $i$-th laser beam with relative angular offset $\alpha_i \in [0, 2\pi)$, the global beam heading is:

$$
\phi_i = \theta_R + \alpha_i
$$

The ray is parameterized by distance $u \in [0, r_{\max}]$:

$$
\mathbf{p}_{\text{ray}}(u) = \begin{bmatrix} x_R \\ y_R \end{bmatrix} + u \begin{bmatrix} \cos\phi_i \\ \sin\phi_i \end{bmatrix}
$$

### Line-Segment Obstacle Intersection
For an obstacle boundary segment connecting endpoints $\mathbf{a} = [x_1, y_1]^T$ and $\mathbf{b} = [x_2, y_2]^T$, parameterized by $v \in [0, 1]$:

$$
\mathbf{p}_{\text{seg}}(v) = \mathbf{a} + v(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})
$$

Equating $\mathbf{p}_{\text{ray}}(u) = \mathbf{p}_{\text{seg}}(v)$ yields a $2 \times 2$ linear system:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\phi_i & -(x_2 - x_1) \\ \sin\phi_i & -(y_2 - y_1) \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 - x_R \\ y_1 - y_R \end{bmatrix}
$$

A valid collision occurs when $0 \le u \le r_{\max}$ and $0 \le v \le 1$. The true distance is the minimum $u$ across all obstacle segments:

$$
d_i = \min_k u_k
$$

---

## 2. Derivation: Point Cloud Extraction with Gaussian Noise

Physical range measurements $r_i$ incorporate random electronic thermal noise $\epsilon_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$:

$$
r_i = d_i + \epsilon_i
$$

Each range reading $(r_i, \alpha_i)$ is converted back into 2D Cartesian **Point Cloud coordinates** in the global map:

$$
\mathbf{p}_i^W = \begin{bmatrix} x_i^W \\ y_i^W \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x_R + r_i \cos(\theta_R + \alpha_i) \\ y_R + r_i \sin(\theta_R + \alpha_i) \end{bmatrix}
$$
