# Sensor Fundamentals, Measurement Models & Gaussian Noise

> "How does a physical robot perceive the real world when every hardware sensor contains inherent measurement uncertainty?"

Without sensors, an autonomous machine is blind. Sensors convert physical environmental phenomena (photons, acoustic pressure waves, magnetic fields, and wheel rotations) into digital numerical signals.

---

## 1. Exteroceptive vs. Proprioceptive Sensors

Robotics classifies perception hardware into two fundamental categories:

1. **Proprioceptive Sensors**: Measure internal states of the robot (e.g., optical wheel encoders, motor current sensors, joint potentiometers, and IMU gyroscopes).
2. **Exteroceptive Sensors**: Measure external environmental features (e.g., 2D/3D LiDAR, ultrasonic rangefinders, stereo cameras, and depth sensors).

---

## 2. The Gaussian Measurement Noise Model

No physical sensor is 100% exact. When a LiDAR measures distance $z_t$ to an obstacle located at true distance $d_{\text{true}}$, the reading is corrupted by additive zero-mean Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$:

$$
z_t = d_{\text{true}} + \epsilon
$$

The conditional probability density function (PDF) representing sensor likelihood is:

$$
p(z_t \mid d_{\text{true}}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} \exp\left( -\frac{(z_t - d_{\text{true}})^2}{2\sigma^2} \right)
$$

Where:
- **$z_t$**: Measured distance reading $[\text{m}]$
- **$d_{\text{true}}$**: Ground-truth obstacle distance $[\text{m}]$
- **$\sigma$**: Sensor standard deviation $[\text{m}]$ ($\sigma^2$ is measurement variance)
