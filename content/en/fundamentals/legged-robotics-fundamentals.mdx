# Legged Robotics: Zero Moment Point (ZMP) & Inverted Pendulum Locomotion

> "Why are humans and bipedal humanoid robots capable of stable walking without tipping over, despite possessing an elevated Center of Mass supported on narrow foot contact polygons?"

**Legged Robotics** (Bipedal Humanoids and Quadrupeds) achieves locomotion through intermittent, non-continuous ground contact states. Dynamic balance is formulated via the **Linear Inverted Pendulum Model (LIPM)** and the classical **Zero Moment Point (ZMP)** criterion introduced by Professor Miomir Vukobratović.

---

## 1. Center of Mass (CoM) vs Zero Moment Point (ZMP)

1. **Center of Mass (CoM $\mathbf{x}_{\text{CoM}}$)**: The spatial weighted average position of all robotic links and actuator masses.
2. **Zero Moment Point (ZMP $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ZMP}}$)**: The precise point on the ground surface where the net horizontal tipping moment (generated by gravity and inertia acceleration) equals zero:

$$
x_{\text{ZMP}} = x_{\text{CoM}} - \frac{z_{\text{CoM}}}{g} \ddot{x}_{\text{CoM}}
$$

$$
y_{\text{ZMP}} = y_{\text{CoM}} - \frac{z_{\text{CoM}}}{g} \ddot{y}_{\text{CoM}}
$$

### Dynamic Stability Criterion:
- **DYNAMICALLY STABLE**: $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ZMP}}$ resides strictly **inside the active foot support polygon**. Ground contact forces remain fully capable of countering inertial tipping moments.
- **UNSTABLE / TIPPING HAZARD**: $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ZMP}}$ hits the edge of the support boundary. Rotational torque begins tipping the foot off the ground.

---

## 2. The Linear Inverted Pendulum Model (LIPM)

By constraining the vertical Center of Mass trajectory to a constant horizontal plane ($z_{\text{CoM}} = z_c$), the non-linear pendulum dynamics linearize cleanly:

$$
\ddot{x}_{\text{CoM}} = \frac{g}{z_c} (x_{\text{CoM}} - x_{\text{ZMP}}) = \omega_0^2 (x_{\text{CoM}} - x_{\text{ZMP}})
$$

Where the pendulum natural eigenfrequency is $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{g}{z_c}}$.
