# Introduction to Robotics & Autonomous Systems

## What is Robotics?

When you hear the word **robot**, you might imagine a humanoid, an industrial arm in an automotive plant, a warehouse automated guided vehicle, or an aerial drone.

Robotics is an interdisciplinary field concerned with building machines that can **sense, compute, and act in the physical world**.

A useful way to think about a robot is:

> A robot is a physical machine that uses sensing, computation, and actuation to perform tasks in the physical world.

The essential idea is that a robot is not simply a computer and not simply a mechanical device. It is a closed-loop system connecting the physical world, sensory perception, and algorithmic computation.

---

## The Sense-Plan-Act Loop

To operate autonomously in dynamic environments, a robot executes the continuous **Sense $\to$ Plan $\to$ Act** cycle:

1. **SENSE**: Perceive the environment using exteroceptive sensors (LiDAR, Cameras, Sonar) and proprioceptive sensors (encoders, IMU).
2. **UNDERSTAND & ESTIMATE**: Fuse sensor data to estimate true state $\mathbf{x}_k = (x, y, \theta)$ and update occupancy grid maps.
3. **PLAN**: Compute collision-free paths using graph search algorithms like A* or trajectory splines.
4. **ACT**: Generate actuator commands (motor PWM voltage, steering servo angles) to drive wheels or limbs.
5. **OBSERVE AGAIN**: Close the feedback loop at frequencies between 10 Hz to 100 Hz.

---

## What Distinguishes a Robot From Simple Automation?

Consider a standard washing machine:
- It has motors, timers, and buttons.
- But it blindly runs fixed pre-programmed cycles regardless of whether clothes are clean, tangled, or overloaded.

Now consider an **autonomous vacuum robot**:
- It observes obstacles, builds a map of the room, plans optimal coverage paths, and recovers when trapped.
- It adapts its actions in response to real-time physical perception.
