# Mathematical Foundations for Robotics

> "Why does a robot need mathematics, and how do mathematical objects become physical motion?"

To build truly autonomous robots, we must bridge the gap between abstract mathematical equations and physical reality in the real world.

---

## 1. Why Mathematics in Robotics?

A human can easily say:
> *"The robot is about two meters to the right of the charging dock."*

However, a digital microprocessor and flight controller require an exact numeric vector to compute motor PWM voltages:

$$
\mathbf{p} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2.0 \\ 1.5 \end{bmatrix} \text{ meters}
$$

Mathematics gives the robot a precise, unambiguous language for describing:
- **Spatial Position & Pose**: Where is the robot and its end-effector located?
- **Orientation & Heading**: Which direction is the robot facing in 2D and 3D?
- **Kinematics & Velocity**: How fast are the wheels rotating and how does the body move?
- **Sensor Uncertainty**: How confident are we in noisy LiDAR distance measurements?

## 2. 2D Coordinate Frames & Cartesian Representation

Before defining vectors, we establish a **reference coordinate frame** $\{W\}$ (World Frame). Any point $P$ in 2D space is represented by its orthogonal projections along the $X$ and $Y$ axes:

$$
P = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}, \quad r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}, \quad \theta = \text{atan2}(y, x)
$$

## 3. Vectors & Pythagorean Decomposition

A **vector** represents both magnitude (length) and spatial direction. Unlike a scalar which only has magnitude ($v = 2\text{ m/s}$, $m = 10\text{ kg}$), a velocity vector tells the robot *where* it is headed:

$$
\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} v_x \\ v_y \end{bmatrix}
$$

By the **Pythagorean Theorem**, the vector magnitude $\|\mathbf{v}\|$ is the hypotenuse of the right-angle triangle formed by $v_x$ and $v_y$:

$$
\|\mathbf{v}\| = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2}
$$

The normalized unit direction vector $\hat{\mathbf{v}}$ is given by:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{v}\|} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta \\ \sin\theta \end{bmatrix}
$$

## 4. Dot Product & Directional Alignment

The **dot product** (scalar product) between two vectors $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ measures their directional alignment:

$$
\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = a_x b_x + a_y b_y = \|\mathbf{a}\| \|\mathbf{b}\| \cos\theta
$$

In robotics, the sign of the dot product is critical:
1. **$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} > 0$ (Acute angle)**: The robot is moving towards the target goal.
2. **$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0$ (Orthogonal / $90^\circ$)**: Pure lateral displacement (perpendicular to goal).
3. **$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} < 0$ (Obtuse angle)**: The robot is moving away from the goal.
