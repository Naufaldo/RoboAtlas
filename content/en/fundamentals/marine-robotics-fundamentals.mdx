# Marine Robotics: Hydrodynamics, Buoyancy & 6-DOF AUV Equations

> "Why are GPS, RF radio, and optical LiDAR virtually unusable underwater, and how do Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs) and Remotely Operated Vehicles (ROVs) achieve stable depth control and navigation using acoustic Doppler sonar and Fossen's equations of motion?"

**Marine Robotics** encompasses **Autonomous Surface Vehicles (USVs)** and **Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs)**. Subsea autonomy operates in an extreme physical regime characterized by massive hydrostatic pressure (increasing by 1 atm every 10 meters of depth), optical darkness, and rapid electromagnetic attenuation that renders high-frequency wireless communication completely ineffective.

## 2. Hydrostatic Equilibrium & Metacentric Passive Stability

A submerged rigid body experiences two primary opposing vertical forces acting at distinct geometric centroids:

$$
W = m g \quad (\text{acting at Center of Gravity } CG = [x_G, y_G, z_G]^T)
$$

$$
B = \rho_{\text{fluid}} g \nabla \quad (\text{acting at Center of Buoyancy } CB = [x_B, y_B, z_B]^T)
$$

Where $\rho_{\text{fluid}}$ is seawater density ($\approx 1025 \text{ kg/m}^3$) and $\nabla = V_{\text{displaced}}$ is the displaced hull fluid volume.

```text
               METACENTRIC PASSIVE STABILITY CONDITION (CB ABOVE CG)
               
                         Buoyancy Force B (Archimedes)
                                    ▲
                                    │
                                 [ CB ] (Center of Buoyancy)
                                    │
                                    │  <-- Restoring Arm (z_G - z_B)
                                    │
                                 [ CG ] (Center of Gravity)
                                    │
                                    ▼
                         Gravitational Weight W
```

### Metacentric Righting Stability Criterion:
To ensure the vehicle is self-righting (passively stable in roll and pitch during power loss), heavy batteries and pressure vessels are positioned at the bottom of the hull while lightweight syntactic buoyancy foam is placed at the top:

$$
z_G > z_B \implies \overline{BG} = z_G - z_B > 0
$$

## 3. SNAME 6-DOF Notation & Underwater Kinematics

The Society of Naval Architects and Marine Engineers (SNAME) standardizes the 6 degrees of freedom:

```text
┌─────────────────┬────────────────────┬────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┐
│ Degree of DOF   │ Position / Angle   │ Body Velocity nu   │ Body Force / Torque tau     │
├─────────────────┼────────────────────┼────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Longitudinal │ x (Surge)          │ u (Surge velocity) │ X (Surge Force)             │
│ 2. Lateral      │ y (Sway)           │ v (Sway velocity)  │ Y (Sway Force)              │
│ 3. Vertical     │ z (Heave)          │ w (Heave velocity) │ Z (Heave Force)             │
│ 4. Rotation X   │ phi (Roll)         │ p (Roll rate)      │ K (Roll Moment)             │
│ 5. Rotation Y   │ theta (Pitch)      │ q (Pitch rate)     │ M (Pitch Moment)            │
│ 6. Rotation Z   │ psi (Yaw)          │ r (Yaw rate)       │ N (Yaw Moment)              │
└─────────────────┴────────────────────┴────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┘
```

The differential kinematic transformation relating body-fixed velocities $\boldsymbol{\nu} = [u, v, w, p, q, r]^T$ to world rates $\boldsymbol{\dot{\eta}} = [\dot{x}, \dot{y}, \dot{z}, \dot{\phi}, \dot{\theta}, \dot{\psi}]^T$ is:

$$
\boldsymbol{\dot{\eta}} = \mathbf{J}(\boldsymbol{\eta}) \boldsymbol{\nu} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{R}_B^I(\boldsymbol{\Theta}) & \mathbf{0}_{3\times 3} \\ \mathbf{0}_{3\times 3} & \mathbf{T}_{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{\Theta}) \end{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{\nu}
$$

## 4. Fossen's 6-DOF Non-Linear Equations of Motion

The universal governing equations for underwater vehicles are formulated in Fossen's matrix representation:

$$
\mathbf{M} \boldsymbol{\dot{\nu}} + \mathbf{C}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\boldsymbol{\nu} + \mathbf{D}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\boldsymbol{\nu} + \mathbf{g}(\boldsymbol{\eta}) = \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{thruster}} + \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{ocean\_current}}
$$

### Matrix Components:
1. **Total System Inertia $\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{M}_{RB} + \mathbf{M}_A$**:
   - $\mathbf{M}_{RB}$: Rigid-body mass and inertia tensor.
   - $\mathbf{M}_A = -\operatorname{diag}(X_{\dot{u}}, Y_{\dot{v}}, Z_{\dot{w}}, K_{\dot{p}}, M_{\dot{q}}, N_{\dot{r}})$: **Hydrodynamic Added Mass**, representing fluid inertia accelerated alongside the hull. In underwater robotics, added mass often equals or exceeds the vehicle's dry mass!
2. **Coriolis & Centripetal Matrix $\mathbf{C}(\boldsymbol{\nu}) = \mathbf{C}_{RB}(\boldsymbol{\nu}) + \mathbf{C}_A(\boldsymbol{\nu})$**.
3. **Hydrodynamic Damping Matrix $\mathbf{D}(\boldsymbol{\nu}) = \mathbf{D}_{\text{linear}} + \mathbf{D}_{\text{nonlinear}}|\boldsymbol{\nu}|$**:
   Capturing laminar skin friction and quadratic turbulent vortex drag $\frac{1}{2} \rho C_D A v^2$.
4. **Hydrostatic Restoring Vector $\mathbf{g}(\boldsymbol{\eta})$**:
   Supplying gravitational righting moments:
   $$g_4(\boldsymbol{\eta}) = (z_G - z_B) W \cos\theta \sin\phi, \quad g_5(\boldsymbol{\eta}) = (z_G - z_B) W \sin\theta$$
