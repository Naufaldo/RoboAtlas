# 2D Planar Geometry & Coordinate Frames

Robotics relies fundamentally on transforming point measurements between multiple coordinate frames (e.g., from a LiDAR sensor frame $\{S\}$ to the robot chassis frame $\{R\}$, and into the global map frame $\{W\}$).

## Homogeneous Transformation Matrix in $SE(2)$

$$\mathbf{p}^W = T_R^W \mathbf{p}^R = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta & t_x \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta & t_y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x^R \\ y^R \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

### Why Homogeneous Coordinates?
In Euclidean geometry, rotation is multiplicative ($\mathbf{p}' = R\mathbf{p}$) while translation is additive ($\mathbf{p}^W = \mathbf{p}' + \mathbf{t}$). By augmenting coordinates with a trailing $1$, both operations combine into a single $3\times 3$ matrix multiplication.
