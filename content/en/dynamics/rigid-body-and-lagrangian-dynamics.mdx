# Rigid Body Dynamics & Lagrangian Mechanics for Robotics

> "While kinematics describes where a robot moves geometrically without considering forces, dynamics reveals the fundamental equation of motion: how motor torques generate joint accelerations against gravity, inertia, and Coriolis forces."

Robot dynamics forms the critical bridge between motion planning and physical actuator control. Whether computing feedforward torques for industrial robot arms, balancing quadrupedal robots, or executing agile drone maneuvers, the **Euler-Lagrange** and **Newton-Euler** formulations provide the mathematical engine governing physical robot motion.

## 1. Lagrangian Mechanics Formulation ($L = K - P$)

Unlike Newtonian mechanics which requires resolving internal constraint forces at every joint pin, the **Lagrangian approach** uses energy scalar fields defined on generalized coordinates $\mathbf{q} = [q_1, q_2, \dots, q_n]^T \in \mathcal{Q}$:

The **Lagrangian function $L$** is defined as the difference between total kinetic energy $K(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}})$ and potential energy $P(\mathbf{q})$:

$$
L(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}}) = K(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}}) - P(\mathbf{q})
$$

### The Euler-Lagrange Equation of Motion:
For each generalized coordinate $q_i$, the dynamic equilibrium equation with external generalized joint torque $\tau_i$ is:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_i} \right) - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i} = \tau_i
$$

## 2. The Standard Robot Manipulator Dynamic Equation

Applying the Euler-Lagrange derivation to an $n$-DOF serial robot arm yields the canonical matrix equation of motion:

$$
M(\mathbf{q})\ddot{\mathbf{q}} + C(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}})\dot{\mathbf{q}} + \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q}) = \boldsymbol{\tau}
$$

Where:
- $M(\mathbf{q}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is the **Inertia Matrix** (symmetric, positive-definite).
- $C(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is the **Coriolis and Centrifugal Matrix** (derived using Christoffel symbols).
- $\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q}) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the **Gravitational Torque Vector** $\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q}) = \frac{\partial P}{\partial \mathbf{q}}$.
- $\boldsymbol{\tau} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the vector of generalized actuator torques/forces.

```text
┌─────────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Dynamic Term            │ Mathematical Symbol  │ Physical Meaning & Key Properties                      │
├─────────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Mass / Inertia Matrix   │ M(q)                 │ Symmetric & positive-definite (q^T M q > 0 for q != 0) │
│ Coriolis / Centrifugal  │ C(q, q_dot) q_dot    │ Velocity-coupled gyroscopic torques (M_dot - 2C skew)  │
│ Gravity Vector          │ g(q)                 │ Conservative static torques required to hold posture   │
│ Actuator Torque         │ tau                  │ Net motor output torque applied at each joint          │
└─────────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The Skew-Symmetry Property of $\dot{M} - 2C$:
A cornerstone of non-linear robot control (such as passivity-based control and Lyapunov stability proofs) is that the matrix:
$$
N(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}}) = \dot{M}(\mathbf{q}) - 2C(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}})
$$
is **skew-symmetric**, meaning $\mathbf{x}^T (\dot{M} - 2C) \mathbf{x} = 0$ for any arbitrary vector $\mathbf{x}$.

## 3. Forward vs. Inverse Dynamics

Robotic autonomy relies on solving this dynamic equation in two opposite directions:

### Inverse Dynamics (Control & Feedforward):
Given desired trajectory $(\mathbf{q}(t), \dot{\mathbf{q}}(t), \ddot{\mathbf{q}}(t))$, compute required motor torques $\boldsymbol{\tau}$:
$$
\boldsymbol{\tau} = M(\mathbf{q})\ddot{\mathbf{q}} + C(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}})\dot{\mathbf{q}} + \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q})
$$
*(Computed in $O(n)$ time using the recursive Newton-Euler algorithm).*

### Forward Dynamics (Simulation & Physics Engines):
Given current state $(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}})$ and applied torques $\boldsymbol{\tau}$, solve for joint accelerations $\ddot{\mathbf{q}}$ to integrate into the next timestep:
$$
\ddot{\mathbf{q}} = M(\mathbf{q})^{-1} \Big( \boldsymbol{\tau} - C(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}})\dot{\mathbf{q}} - \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q}) \Big)
$$
