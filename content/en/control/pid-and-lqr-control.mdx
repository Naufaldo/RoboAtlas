# PID Feedback Control & State-Space Optimal Regulation

> "When external wind gusts, friction, and motor disturbances knock a robot off course, how does a feedback controller actively restore equilibrium?"

Open-loop commands fail in physical environments. **Feedback control** measures the actual error between desired reference state $r(t)$ and measured state $y(t)$, continuously generating corrective control action $u(t)$.

## 1. The Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) Control Law

PID calculates control effort $u(t)$ from error signal $e(t) = r(t) - y(t)$:

$$
u(t) = K_p \, e(t) + K_i \int_0^t e(\tau) \, d\tau + K_d \, \frac{de(t)}{dt}
$$

### Individual Component Actions:
1. **Proportional Term ($K_p e(t)$)**: Generates restoring force proportional to current error. Increases responsiveness and rise time.
2. **Integral Term ($K_i \int e(\tau) d\tau$)**: Accumulates past persistent errors over time, eliminating **steady-state offset error**.
3. **Derivative Term ($K_d \frac{de}{dt}$)**: Anticipates future error trajectory and provides damping, reducing **overshoot** and oscillations.

## 2. Discrete-Time Implementation for Microcontrollers

In embedded robot microcontrollers operating at discrete control loops with timestep $\Delta t$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
e_k &= r_k - y_k \\
I_k &= I_{k-1} + e_k \Delta t \\
D_k &= \frac{e_k - e_{k-1}}{\Delta t} \\
u_k &= K_p e_k + K_i I_k + K_d D_k
\end{aligned}
$$

*(Anti-windup clamping is applied to prevent integral term saturation).*
