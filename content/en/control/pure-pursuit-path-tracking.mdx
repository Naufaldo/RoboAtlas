# Pure Pursuit Geometric Path Tracking Control (M13)

> "How can a simple geometric lookahead circle guide an autonomous vehicle smoothly along complex sharp curves without heavy optimization solvers?"

**Pure Pursuit** is one of the most widely implemented trajectory tracking algorithms in mobile robotics, planetary rovers, and autonomous racecars.

---

## 1. Geometric Lookahead & Arc Derivation

Let the rear-axle of the vehicle be at position $\mathbf{p}_R = [x_R, y_R]^T$ with heading $\theta_R$.

The algorithm places a **lookahead circle** of radius $L_d$ centered at the vehicle rear axle. It finds the intersection point $\mathbf{g} = [g_x, g_y]^T$ on the planned path at distance $L_d$:

```text
               Lookahead Target g(gx, gy)
                     *
                    / \
                   /   \
                  /  R  \
                 /       \
      Robot (x, y) ──────── Center of Arc (ICC)
            \      alpha  /
             \───────────/
```

### 1.1 Angle to Target ($\alpha$)
The angle between vehicle heading $\theta_R$ and the vector pointing to target $\mathbf{g}$:

$$
\alpha = \text{atan2}(g_y - y_R, g_x - x_R) - \theta_R
$$

### 1.2 Arc Curvature Formula ($\kappa$)
By the Law of Sines on the isosceles triangle formed by the circle arc radius $R$:

$$
\frac{L_d}{\sin(2\alpha)} = \frac{R}{\sin(90^\circ - \alpha)} = \frac{R}{\cos\alpha}
$$

Using $\sin(2\alpha) = 2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha$:

$$
\frac{L_d}{2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha} = \frac{R}{\cos\alpha} \implies R = \frac{L_d}{2\sin\alpha}
$$

Thus, the requested path curvature $\kappa$ is:

$$
\kappa = \frac{1}{R} = \frac{2\sin\alpha}{L_d}
$$

### 1.3 Front Wheel Steering Angle ($\delta$)
For an Ackermann / bicycle vehicle with wheelbase $L$:

$$
\delta = \arctan(\kappa \cdot L) = \arctan\left( \frac{2L\sin\alpha}{L_d} \right)
$$

---

## 2. Tuning Lookahead Distance $L_d$ Trade-Offs

The lookahead distance $L_d$ governs the behavior of the controller:

1. **Small $L_d$ ($L_d \to 0$)**: Highly aggressive tracking. Quickly corrects cross-track error but induces steering overshoots and high-frequency oscillations.
2. **Large $L_d$**: Extremely stable, smooth steering. However, the vehicle "cuts corners" on tight turns and exhibits sluggish error correction.
3. **Speed-Scaled Lookahead**: In practice, $L_d$ is scaled dynamically with forward velocity $v$:
   $$
   L_d(v) = k_{\text{gain}} \cdot v + L_{d,\min}
   $$
