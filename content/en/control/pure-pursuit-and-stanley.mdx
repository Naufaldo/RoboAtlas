# Pure Pursuit & Stanley Trajectory Tracking Control

> "Once a path planning algorithm gives the robot a list of waypoint coordinates, how do the steering servos continuously guide the chassis without oscillating off course?"

Trajectory tracking controllers bridge the gap between global discrete geometry and real-time continuous steering actuators.

---

## 1. The Pure Pursuit Algorithm

Pure Pursuit is a geometric path tracking method that calculates the steering angle required to chase a **lookahead target point** on the reference path located at a distance $L_f$ ahead of the rear axle.

### Geometric Arc Derivation:
By constructing a circular arc of radius $R$ connecting the rear axle $(x_r, y_r)$ to the lookahead point $(g_x, g_y)$:

$$
\frac{L_f}{\sin(2\alpha)} = \frac{R}{\sin(\frac{\pi}{2} - \alpha)} \implies R = \frac{L_f}{2\sin\alpha}
$$

Using the kinematic bicycle model with wheelbase $L$ where $\kappa = \frac{1}{R} = \frac{\tan\delta}{L}$:

$$
\delta = \arctan\left( \frac{2 L \sin\alpha}{L_f} \right)
$$

### Tuning the Lookahead Distance $L_f$:
- **Velocity-Scaled Lookahead**: $L_f = k_v \cdot v + L_{f,0}$
- **Small $L_f$**: Fast response, aggressive turning, but causes high-frequency oscillations.
- **Large $L_f$**: Extremely smooth steering, but cuts corners at sharp turns.

---

## 2. The Stanley Controller

Developed by Stanford University for *Stanley* (the autonomous vehicle that won the DARPA Grand Challenge), the Stanley controller measures tracking errors at the **front axle** rather than the rear:

$$
\delta(t) = \theta_e(t) + \arctan\left( \frac{k \cdot e(t)}{v(t)} \right)
$$

### Components:
1. **Heading Error $\theta_e(t) = \theta_{\text{path}} - \theta_{\text{robot}}$**: Steers the wheels parallel to the reference path tangent.
2. **Cross-Track Error $e(t)$**: The shortest perpendicular distance from the front axle to the path.
3. **Gain $k > 0$**: Convergence gain scaling responsiveness.
4. **Softening Factor $v(t) \to v(t) + k_{\text{soft}}$**: Prevents division by zero at low speeds or stops.

### Why Stanley is Asymptotically Stable:
When heading error is zero ($\theta_e = 0$), the time derivative of cross-track error $\dot{e}(t) = -v\sin\delta = -v \sin\left(\arctan\frac{ke}{v}\right) = \frac{-k e}{\sqrt{1 + (ke/v)^2}}$. For small errors, $\dot{e}(t) \approx -k e(t)$, yielding exponential decay $e(t) = e_0 e^{-kt}$.
