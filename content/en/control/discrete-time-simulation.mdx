# Discrete-Time Numerical Simulation: Euler, RK4 & Stability (M28)

> "Robots obey continuous laws of physics, but digital microcontrollers execute algorithms in discrete time steps (dt). What causes numerical simulation to drift or explode?"

In digital computers, continuous differential equations $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u})$ cannot be integrated infinitely fast. They are converted into **discrete difference equations** executed at fixed intervals $\Delta t$.

---

## 1. Discretization Solvers Taxonomy

Given the initial value problem $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = f(\mathbf{x}, t)$ with step size $\Delta t$:

### 1.1 Forward Euler Method (First-Order $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t)$)
$$
\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{x}_k + \Delta t \cdot f(\mathbf{x}_k, t_k)
$$
- Simplest to compute (1 function evaluation per step).
- Accumulates local truncation error $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^2)$ and global error $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t)$.

### 1.2 Runge-Kutta 4th Order / RK4 (Fourth-Order $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^4)$)
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{k}_1 &= f(\mathbf{x}_k, t_k) \\
\mathbf{k}_2 &= f\left(\mathbf{x}_k + \frac{\Delta t}{2}\mathbf{k}_1, \, t_k + \frac{\Delta t}{2}\right) \\
\mathbf{k}_3 &= f\left(\mathbf{x}_k + \frac{\Delta t}{2}\mathbf{k}_2, \, t_k + \frac{\Delta t}{2}\right) \\
\mathbf{k}_4 &= f\left(\mathbf{x}_k + \Delta t \mathbf{k}_3, \, t_k + \Delta t\right) \\
\mathbf{x}_{k+1} &= \mathbf{x}_k + \frac{\Delta t}{6}(\mathbf{k}_1 + 2\mathbf{k}_2 + 2\mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4)
\end{aligned}
$$
- Standard workhorse for robotics simulations (e.g. Gazebo, MuJoCo, Isaac Sim).

---

## 2. Numerical Stability Limits & Energy Drift

For a linear oscillator $\dot{x} = \lambda x$ ($\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$):

Applying Forward Euler gives:

$$
x_{k+1} = (1 + \lambda \Delta t) x_k
$$

The numerical integration is **stable** if and only if:

$$
|1 + \lambda \Delta t| \le 1
$$

For pure oscillatory systems ($\lambda = \pm j\omega$), Forward Euler has $|1 \pm j\omega\Delta t| = \sqrt{1 + \omega^2\Delta t^2} > 1$, which **artificially injects numerical energy on every step**, causing the amplitude to diverge exponentially!
