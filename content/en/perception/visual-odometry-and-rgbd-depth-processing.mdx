# Visual Odometry, Epipolar Geometry & 3D RGB-D Point Cloud Processing

> "How can an autonomous robot determine its exact 6-DOF camera trajectory incrementally solely by comparing consecutive image frames, and how do RGB-D depth sensors transform 2D pixel grids into dense 3D point cloud metric environments?"

**Visual Odometry (VO)** is the process of estimating the 6-DOF ego-motion $[R | \mathbf{t}] \in SE(3)$ of a robot using sequential camera images. By tracking salient visual keypoints (ORB, SIFT, FAST) and enforcing geometric **Epipolar Constraints**, VO provides high-rate pose estimates critical for GPS-denied navigation in autonomous cars, drones, and planetary rovers (Mars Curiosity and Perseverance).

## 1. Two-View Epipolar Geometry & The Essential Matrix ($E$)

Consider a 3D physical point $\mathbf{P}$ viewed by two calibrated camera poses $\{C_1\}$ and $\{C_2\}$ separated by relative rotation $R$ and translation $\mathbf{t}$:

```text
         Camera 1 (C_1)                      Camera 2 (C_2)
              ▲                                   ▲
             / \                                 / \
            /   \                               /   \
           /  x1 \                             / x2  \
          /   ●   \                           /   ●   \
         O─────────O                         O─────────O
          \                                             /
           \                                           /
            \                                         /
             ───────────────► ● P ◄───────────────────
                           (3D Point)
```

The camera centers $C_1, C_2$ and point $\mathbf{P}$ define the **Epipolar Plane**. Normalized image coordinates $\mathbf{x}_1 = K^{-1} \mathbf{p}_1$ and $\mathbf{x}_2 = K^{-1} \mathbf{p}_2$ satisfy the **Epipolar Constraint**:

$$
\mathbf{x}_2^T E \, \mathbf{x}_1 = 0
$$

Where $E \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ is the **Essential Matrix**, defined as:

$$
E = [\mathbf{t}]_\times R = \begin{bmatrix}
0 & -t_z & t_y \\
t_z & 0 & -t_x \\
-t_y & t_x & 0
\end{bmatrix} R
$$

### The Fundamental Matrix ($F$ for Uncalibrated Images):
When operating directly on raw pixel coordinates $(u, v)$ without knowing intrinsics $K$:

$$
\mathbf{p}_2^T F \, \mathbf{p}_1 = 0, \quad \text{where } F = K_2^{-T} E \, K_1^{-1}
$$

## 2. Recovering Camera Motion: The 8-Point Algorithm & SVD

Given $N \ge 8$ matched keypoint correspondences $\{\mathbf{x}_{1,i} \leftrightarrow \mathbf{x}_{2,i}\}$ filtered with RANSAC:
1. Construct the linear measurement matrix $A \mathbf{e} = \mathbf{0}$ and solve using SVD ($A = U_A \Sigma_A V_A^T$).
2. Enforce the rank-2 constraint on $E$ by setting the smallest singular value to zero: $E = U \, \text{diag}(\sigma, \sigma, 0) \, V^T$.
3. Extract relative motion $[R | \mathbf{t}]$ from $E$ via SVD factorization:
   $$R = U W V^T \quad \text{or} \quad U W^T V^T, \quad \text{where } W = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
   $$[\mathbf{t}]_\times = U Z U^T, \quad \text{where } Z = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Of the 4 possible mathematical decompositions, the unique physical solution is identified through **Cheirality Checking** (ensuring triangulated 3D points lie strictly in front of both cameras, $Z_1 > 0, Z_2 > 0$).

## 3. 3D RGB-D Depth Sensor Back-Projection

Structured-light and Time-of-Flight (ToF) cameras (Intel RealSense, Azure Kinect) provide synchronized RGB image $I(u, v)$ and metric depth map $Z(u, v)$. 

Every pixel $(u, v)$ with measured depth $Z$ back-projects into a metric 3D point $\mathbf{P}_C = [X, Y, Z]^T$ in camera coordinates:

$$
\mathbf{P}_C = Z \cdot K^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
\frac{(u - c_x) Z}{f_x} \\
\frac{(v - c_y) Z}{f_y} \\
Z
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Transforming the point cloud into the global robot map frame using extrinsic sensor calibration $[R_{CB} | \mathbf{t}_{CB}]$:

$$
\mathbf{P}_{\text{robot}} = R_{CB} \, \mathbf{P}_C + \mathbf{t}_{CB}
$$
