# Gaussian Grid Mapping & Kernel Density Estimation

> "How can a robot accommodate the physical uncertainty of laser returns without immediately collapsing space into rigid binary obstacle cells?"

In physical environments, laser range measurements contain inherent uncertainty caused by electronic noise, optical divergence, and oblique surface incidence angles. **Gaussian Grid Mapping** represents each laser endpoint not as a single discrete cell, but as a continuous **2D isotropic Gaussian probability density kernel (Kernel Density Estimation / KDE)** that deposits smooth probability masses onto surrounding spatial cells.

![Gaussian Grid Mapping in Robotics](/images/sensors/gaussian-grid-map.jpg)

---

## 1. Mathematical Formulation: 2D Gaussian Kernel

When a range sensor records a collision endpoint at global Cartesian coordinate $(x_0, y_0)$, the probability density contribution at arbitrary cell coordinate $(x, y)$ is evaluated using an isotropic bivariate normal distribution with variance $\sigma^2$:

$$
P(x, y) = \frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^2} \exp\left( -\frac{(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2}{2\sigma^2} \right)
$$

### Grid Density Accumulation:
For a discretized grid world with spatial resolution $\Delta x$, the cumulative occupancy score at grid cell $(i, j)$ after $K$ laser returns is given by:

$$
M(i, j) = \min\left(1.0, \sum_{k=1}^K P_k(x_i, y_j)\right)
$$

Where:
- $\sigma$ governs the spatial dispersion bandwidth of sensor uncertainty across neighboring cells.
- The center cell receives maximum density, decaying smoothly and exponentially outward.

---

## 2. Key Advantages of Gaussian Grid Maps

1. **Continuous Differentiability**: Gaussian density maps form smooth potential energy surfaces, enabling gradient-based motion planners and trajectory optimizers to compute continuous gradients without discrete step discontinuities.
2. **Robustness to Isolated Noise**: Stray specular reflections or transient dust particles do not create instant impenetrable barriers, requiring repeated multi-beam reinforcement.
3. **Implicit Safety Inflation**: The $\sigma$ variance parameter inherently functions as a natural safety buffer around obstacle perimeters.
