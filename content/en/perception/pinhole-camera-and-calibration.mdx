# Pinhole Camera Model, Perspective Projection & Intrinsic Calibration

> "How does a robot camera map photons bouncing off 3D objects in the physical world into discrete 2D pixel coordinates $(u, v)$ on a digital sensor array?"

Vision is arguably the richest sensing modality in autonomous robotics. Whether guiding self-driving cars, enabling Visual SLAM (ORB-SLAM / FAST-LIO), or detecting graspable objects for robotic arms, the **Pinhole Camera Model** establishes the fundamental projective geometry bridging continuous 3D Euclidean space and 2D digital image planes.

## 1. Geometric Perspective Projection

Under ideal pinhole projection without optical lenses, light rays from a 3D physical point $\mathbf{P}_C = [X_C, Y_C, Z_C]^T$ in the camera optical frame pass through an infinitesimally small aperture (*the pinhole*) and project onto an image plane located at focal length $f$ along the optical $Z$-axis:

$$
x = f \frac{X_C}{Z_C}, \quad y = f \frac{Y_C}{Z_C}
$$

Notice that perspective projection introduces non-linear perspective division by depth $Z_C$. Points further away project closer to the optical center.

---

## 2. Intrinsic Camera Calibration Matrix ($K$)

To convert continuous normalized image coordinates $(x, y)$ in metric units (meters or millimeters) into discrete digital pixel coordinates $(u, v)$ with pixel scaling factors $m_x, m_y$ (pixels/mm) and principal point $(c_x, c_y)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
u &= f_x \frac{X_C}{Z_C} + c_x \\
v &= f_y \frac{Y_C}{Z_C} + c_y
\end{aligned}
$$

Where $f_x = f \cdot m_x$ and $f_y = f \cdot m_y$ denote the effective focal lengths in pixel units. In homogeneous coordinates, this linear projective relationship is expressed compactly via the **Camera Intrinsic Matrix $K$**:

$$
s \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = K \begin{bmatrix} X_C \\ Y_C \\ Z_C \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} f_x & s_{xy} & c_x \\ 0 & f_y & c_y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} X_C \\ Y_C \\ Z_C \end{bmatrix}
$$

*(where $s_{xy}$ is the skew coefficient, typically 0 for rectangular modern CMOS/CCD sensors, and $s = Z_C$ is the projective scale factor).*

---

## 3. The Complete Projective Pipeline: Extrinsics + Intrinsics

When the 3D landmark $\mathbf{P}_W = [X_W, Y_W, Z_W, 1]^T$ is specified in the global world frame $\{W\}$, it must first be transformed into camera frame $\{C\}$ via the **Extrinsic Matrix $[R | \mathbf{t}]$** before applying intrinsic projection:

$$
s \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} f_x & 0 & c_x \\ 0 & f_y & c_y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}_{K \text{ (Intrinsics, } 3\times3\text{)}} \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} R_{11} & R_{12} & R_{13} & t_x \\ R_{21} & R_{22} & R_{23} & t_y \\ R_{31} & R_{32} & R_{33} & t_z \end{bmatrix}}_{[R|\mathbf{t}] \text{ (Extrinsics, } 3\times4\text{)}} \begin{bmatrix} X_W \\ Y_W \\ Z_W \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

The combined matrix $P = K [R | \mathbf{t}] \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 4}$ is the **Full Camera Projection Matrix**.

## 4. Lens Distortion: Radial & Tangential Models

Real-world optical glass lenses introduce geometric distortions (barrel, pincushion, and sensor misalignment):

### Radial Distortion (Brown-Conrady Model):
$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{\text{dist}} &= x (1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4 + k_3 r^6) \\
y_{\text{dist}} &= y (1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4 + k_3 r^6)
\end{aligned}
$$
where $r^2 = x^2 + y^2$.

### Tangential Distortion (Non-parallel lens-sensor plane):
$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{\text{dist}} &= x_{\text{radial}} + [2 p_1 x y + p_2 (r^2 + 2x^2)] \\
y_{\text{dist}} &= y_{\text{radial}} + [p_1 (r^2 + 2y^2) + 2 p_2 x y]
\end{aligned}
$$

Camera calibration routines (e.g., OpenCV `cv2.calibrateCamera` using planar checkerboards) estimate the 5 distortion coefficients $\mathbf{D} = [k_1, k_2, p_1, p_2, k_3]$ and intrinsic matrix $K$.
