# LiDAR to Grid Map Polar-to-Cartesian Conversion

> "How does a raw LiDAR sensor stream of range-bearing pairs (r, θ) transform into a globally consistent 2D discrete spatial obstacle grid?"

LiDAR hardware acquires environmental measurements in **polar sensor coordinates**: range $r_i$ at azimuth scan angle $\theta_i$. To integrate these readings for autonomous path planning and obstacle avoidance, data must undergo a **three-stage sequential transformation pipeline**:
1. Polar to Local Cartesian Conversion
2. Rigid Body $SE(2)$ Transform to Global World Coordinates
3. Spatial Discretization (*Grid Binning*) into Matrix Cells

![LiDAR to Grid Map Conversion Pipeline](/images/sensors/lidar-to-grid-map.jpg)

---

## 1. The Three-Stage Transformation Pipeline

### Stage 1: Polar to Local Sensor Frame $\{L\}$
For each $i$-th laser beam return:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} x_i^L \\ y_i^L \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} r_i \cos\theta_i \\ r_i \sin\theta_i \end{bmatrix}
$$

### Stage 2: Rigid Body $SE(2)$ Transformation to World Frame $\{W\}$
Using the estimated robot pose $(x_R, y_R, \theta_R)$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} x_i^W \\ y_i^W \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_R & -\sin\theta_R \\ \sin\theta_R & \cos\theta_R \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_i^L \\ y_i^L \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} x_R \\ y_R \end{bmatrix}
$$

### Stage 3: Spatial Discretization (*Grid Binning*)
Given grid cell resolution $\Delta x$ and map origin $(X_{\text{origin}}, Y_{\text{origin}})$:

$$
i = \left\lfloor \frac{x_i^W - X_{\text{origin}}}{\Delta x} \right\rfloor, \quad j = \left\lfloor \frac{y_i^W - Y_{\text{origin}}}{\Delta y} \right\rfloor
$$

Each cell accumulates hits: $\text{Grid}[j][i] = \text{Grid}[j][i] + 1$.
