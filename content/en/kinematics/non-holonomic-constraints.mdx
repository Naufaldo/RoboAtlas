# Non-Holonomic Motion Constraints & Pfaffian Forms (M07)

> "Why can't a differential-drive robot or car slide directly sideways into a tight parking spot, even though it can eventually park there through a sequence of maneuvers?"

A mechanical constraint on a robotic system is classified as either **holonomic** (constrains position coordinates directly, $f(q) = 0$) or **non-holonomic** (constrains instantaneous velocity directions without restricting long-term reachable positions, $A(q)\dot{q} = 0$).

---

## 1. Derivation: The Lateral No-Slip Wheel Constraint

For a differential drive robot or standard car, the wheels roll without slipping along their longitudinal direction, but **cannot slip sideways**:

In the robot body frame $\{R\}$, the lateral velocity must be strictly zero:

$$
\dot{y}_R = 0
$$

Transforming the global velocities $(\dot{x}, \dot{y})$ into the robot local frame via rotation matrix $R(-\theta)$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{x}_R \\ \dot{y}_R \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \dot{x} \\ \dot{y} \end{bmatrix}
$$

Setting the second row $\dot{y}_R = 0$ yields the canonical **Pfaffian Velocity Constraint**:

$$
-\dot{x}\sin\theta + \dot{y}\cos\theta = 0
$$

In standard matrix form $A(q)\dot{q} = 0$ where $q = [x, y, \theta]^T$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} -\sin\theta & \cos\theta & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \dot{x} \\ \dot{y} \\ \dot{\theta} \end{bmatrix} = 0
$$

---

## 2. Non-Integrability & Controllability (Lie Brackets)

This constraint cannot be integrated into a function of configuration coordinates alone:

$$
\int (-\sin\theta \, dx + \cos\theta \, dy) \neq f(x, y, \theta)
$$

### 2.1 The Control Vector Fields
The system is steered by two independent control inputs $u = [v, \omega]^T$:

$$
\dot{q} = g_1(q) v + g_2(q) \omega = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta \\ \sin\theta \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} v + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \omega
$$

### 2.2 The Lie Bracket (Parallel Parking Vector Field)
Computing the Lie bracket $[g_1, g_2] = \frac{\partial g_2}{\partial q} g_1 - \frac{\partial g_1}{\partial q} g_2$:

$$
[g_1, g_2] = \begin{bmatrix} \sin\theta \\ -\cos\theta \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}
$$

$[g_1, g_2]$ points **directly sideways**! By oscillating between forward motion ($g_1$) and turning ($g_2$), the robot generates sideways displacement (the classical parallel parking maneuver). By the **Chow-Rashevsky Theorem**, the robot is completely controllable in $\mathbb{R}^2 \times S^1$.
