# Denavit-Hartenberg (DH) Parameters & Serial Manipulator Forward Kinematics

> "How do industrial robotic arms with 6 or more rotating revolute and prismatic joints establish a universally standardized coordinate transformation chain from the fixed base to the end-effector gripper?"

In serial multi-link robot manipulators, establishing arbitrary coordinate frames on every link quickly leads to geometric confusion and redundant parameterizations. In 1955, Jacques Denavit and Richard Hartenberg introduced a compact, minimal convention that attaches coordinate frames to successive robot joints using only **4 geometric parameters per link**.

## 1. The Four Canonical DH Parameters

Between two successive coordinate frames $\{i-1\}$ and $\{i\}$, the spatial relationship is completely described by four scalar values:

```text
┌───────────────────────────────┬────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Parameter Name                │ Symbol     │ Geometric Definition along / around Joint Axes                         │
├───────────────────────────────┼────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Link Length (a_i)          │ a_i        │ Distance along x_i axis from z_{i-1} to z_i (Common normal distance)  │
│ 2. Link Twist (alpha_i)       │ \alpha_i   │ Angle of rotation around x_i axis from z_{i-1} to z_i                  │
│ 3. Link Offset (d_i)          │ d_i        │ Distance along z_{i-1} axis from x_{i-1} to x_i (Joint variable for P)│
│ 4. Joint Angle (theta_i)      │ \theta_i   │ Angle of rotation around z_{i-1} axis from x_{i-1} to x_i (Var for R) │
└───────────────────────────────┴────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- For **Revolute (R) joints**: $\theta_i$ is the active controlled joint variable, while $a_i, \alpha_i, d_i$ are constant physical link dimensions.
- For **Prismatic (P) joints**: $d_i$ is the active joint displacement variable, while $a_i, \alpha_i, \theta_i$ are constant geometric properties.

## 2. Standard Homogeneous Transformation Matrix ($A_i$)

The relative homogeneous transformation matrix $T_{i-1, i}$ representing frame $\{i\}$ relative to frame $\{i-1\}$ is evaluated as a sequence of four fundamental spatial transformations:

$$
T_{i-1, i} = \text{Rot}_z(\theta_i) \, \text{Trans}_z(d_i) \, \text{Trans}_x(a_i) \, \text{Rot}_x(\alpha_i)
$$

Multiplying these four elementary matrices yields the general $4 \times 4$ Denavit-Hartenberg transformation matrix:

$$
T_{i-1, i} = \begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta_i & -\sin\theta_i\cos\alpha_i & \sin\theta_i\sin\alpha_i & a_i\cos\theta_i \\
\sin\theta_i & \cos\theta_i\cos\alpha_i & -\cos\theta_i\sin\alpha_i & a_i\sin\theta_i \\
0 & \sin\alpha_i & \cos\alpha_i & d_i \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

---

## 3. Forward Kinematics Chain ($T_0^n$)

For an $n$-degree-of-freedom serial robot arm, the total forward kinematics transform from the stationary robot base frame $\{0\}$ to the end-effector tool frame $\{n\}$ is computed by chained post-multiplication:

$$
T_0^n(\mathbf{q}) = T_0^1(q_1) \cdot T_1^2(q_2) \cdot T_2^3(q_3) \cdots T_{n-1}^n(q_n) = \begin{bmatrix} R_0^n(\mathbf{q}) & \mathbf{p}_0^n(\mathbf{q}) \\ \mathbf{0}_{1\times3} & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

Where:
- $\mathbf{q} = [q_1, q_2, \dots, q_n]^T$ is the joint configuration vector in joint space $\mathcal{Q}$.
- $R_0^n \in SO(3)$ is the orientation matrix of the end-effector tool.
- $\mathbf{p}_0^n = [x, y, z]^T \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the global Cartesian position of the tool tip.

## 5. Standard DH Frame Assignment Rules

To construct a valid DH parameter table for any serial arm:
1. Align the $z_i$ axis along the axis of revolution (or axis of translation for prismatic joints) of joint $i+1$.
2. Locate the origin $O_i$ at the intersection of the $z_i$ axis and the common normal between $z_{i-1}$ and $z_i$.
3. Direct the $x_i$ axis along the common normal from $z_{i-1}$ to $z_i$. If the axes intersect, choose $x_i \perp \text{span}(z_{i-1}, z_i)$.
4. Complete the right-handed coordinate frame by setting $y_i = z_i \times x_i$.
