# Differential Drive Kinematics & Instantaneous Center of Curvature (M06)

> "How do independent rotational speeds of two parallel motorized wheels translate into the 2D linear trajectory and turning curvature of a mobile robot?"

The **differential drive unicycle** is the most widely deployed chassis configuration in autonomous mobile robotics (AGVs, warehouse robots, robotic vacuum cleaners, and educational rovers).

---

## 1. Forward Kinematic Equations

Let $r$ be the wheel radius, $L$ the axle track width (baseline distance between wheels), and $\omega_L, \omega_R$ the angular velocities of the left and right wheels:

### 1.1 Individual Wheel Linear Velocities
$$
v_L = r \cdot \omega_L, \qquad v_R = r \cdot \omega_R
$$

### 1.2 Robot Chassis Linear and Angular Velocities
The robot chassis midpoint forward speed $v$ and counter-clockwise rotational velocity $\omega$ are:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= \frac{v_R + v_L}{2} = \frac{r}{2}(\omega_R + \omega_L) \\
\omega &= \frac{v_R - v_L}{L} = \frac{r}{L}(\omega_R - \omega_L)
\end{aligned}
$$

### 1.3 State Propagation in World Coordinates
In global Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, \theta)$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{x} \\ \dot{y} \\ \dot{\theta} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & 0 \\ \sin\theta & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} v \\ \omega \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{r}{2}(\omega_R + \omega_L)\cos\theta \\ \frac{r}{2}(\omega_R + \omega_L)\sin\theta \\ \frac{r}{L}(\omega_R - \omega_L) \end{bmatrix}
$$

---

## 2. Derivation: Instantaneous Center of Curvature (ICC)

When a differential robot turns ($\omega \neq 0$), both wheels must rotate around a common instantaneous pivot point called the **Instantaneous Center of Curvature (ICC)** lying on the common wheel axis.

The turn radius $R$ from the chassis center to the ICC is:

$$
R = \frac{v}{\omega} = \frac{L}{2} \left( \frac{v_R + v_L}{v_R - v_L} \right)
$$

The global coordinates of the ICC are:

$$
\text{ICC} = \begin{bmatrix} x - R \sin\theta \\ y + R \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}
$$

### Three Canonical Operating Regimes:
1. **$v_L = v_R$**: $\omega = 0, R = \infty \implies$ **Pure straight motion**.
2. **$v_L = -v_R$**: $v = 0, R = 0 \implies$ **In-place point turn**.
3. **$v_L = 0, v_R > 0$**: $R = L/2 \implies$ **Pivot turn about the stationary left wheel**.
