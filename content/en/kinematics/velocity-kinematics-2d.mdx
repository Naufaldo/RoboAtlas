# 2D Velocity Kinematics & Differential-Drive Modeling

> "How does spinning two independent wheels at different speeds translate into smooth curvilinear trajectory tracking in the plane?"

The differential-drive chassis (found in warehouse AGVs, autonomous vacuum cleaners, and planetary rovers) is the most widespread mobile robot locomotion architecture in robotics.

---

## 1. Physical Anatomy & Motion Parameters

A differential-drive robot consists of two coaxial motorized drive wheels separated by a track gauge (wheelbase) $L$, and one or two passive caster wheels for balance:

- **$v_L$**: Linear velocity of the left wheel ($v_L = r \omega_L$) $[\text{m/s}]$
- **$v_R$**: Linear velocity of the right wheel ($v_R = r \omega_R$) $[\text{m/s}]$
- **$L$**: Distance between wheel contact points (track width) $[\text{m}]$
- **$r$**: Wheel radius $[\text{m}]$

---

## 2. Forward Velocity Kinematics Mapping

The chassis body motion is represented by linear forward speed $v$ and rotational angular velocity $\omega$:

### Linear Velocity $v$:
The robot moves forward at the average speed of both drive wheels:

$$
v = \frac{v_R + v_L}{2}
$$

### Angular Velocity $\omega$:
The robot rotates because of the differential velocity across baseline $L$:

$$
\omega = \frac{v_R - v_L}{L}
$$

### Matrix Formulation:
$$
\begin{bmatrix} v \\ \omega \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{2} \\ \frac{1}{L} & -\frac{1}{L} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} v_R \\ v_L \end{bmatrix}
$$

---

## 3. Instantaneous Center of Curvature (ICC)

When $v_L \neq v_R$, the robot follows a circular arc around a common point called the **Instantaneous Center of Curvature (ICC)**.

The radius of curvature $R_{ICC}$ from the robot center to the ICC is:

$$
R_{ICC} = \frac{v}{\omega} = \frac{L}{2} \left( \frac{v_R + v_L}{v_R - v_L} \right)
$$

### Special Locomotion Cases:
1. **$v_L = v_R$**: $R_{ICC} \to \infty, \omega = 0 \implies$ Pure straight-line forward motion.
2. **$v_L = -v_R$**: $R_{ICC} = 0, v = 0 \implies$ Pure in-place pivot rotation (*zero turning radius*).
3. **$v_L = 0, v_R > 0$**: $R_{ICC} = +L/2 \implies$ Pivot around stationary left wheel.

---

## 4. The Non-Holonomic No-Slip Constraint

A differential-drive robot cannot slide sideways instantaneously. Under ideal rolling without lateral skidding, the lateral velocity in the robot frame $\{R\}$ is strictly zero:

$$
\dot{y}^R = 0 \implies -\dot{x}\sin\theta + \dot{y}\cos\theta = 0
$$

This is a **Pfaffian non-holonomic constraint**. While the robot can reach any configuration $(x, y, \theta)$ in 2D space, it cannot move in arbitrary velocity directions instantaneously.

---

## 5. Continuous-Time State Evolution (Unicycle Model)

In the global world frame $\{W\}$, the kinematic state $\mathbf{x} = [x, y, \theta]^T$ evolves according to:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{x} \\ \dot{y} \\ \dot{\theta} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & 0 \\ \sin\theta & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} v \\ \omega \end{bmatrix}
$$

Using numerical Euler integration with timestep $\Delta t$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{k+1} &= x_k + v_k \cos\theta_k \, \Delta t \\
y_{k+1} &= y_k + v_k \sin\theta_k \, \Delta t \\
\theta_{k+1} &= \theta_k + \omega_k \, \Delta t
\end{aligned}
$$

## 7. Inverse Kinematics Mapping

When a high-level path follower (like Pure Pursuit) outputs desired body commands $(v, \omega)$, the low-level motor controllers compute required wheel velocities:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_R &= v + \frac{\omega L}{2} \\
v_L &= v - \frac{\omega L}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
