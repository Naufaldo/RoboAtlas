# 3D Spatial Geometry, Euler Angles & $SO(3)$ Rotations

> "Why does the order in which a robot rotates in 3D change where it ends up pointing?"

In planar 2D robotics, orientation is simple: a single scalar angle $\theta$. But in 3D physical space—whether positioning a 6-DOF industrial arm end-effector, balancing an aerial drone, or orienting a submarine ROV—orientation requires three rotational degrees of freedom.

---

## 1. Elementary 3D Rotation Matrices

Rotation around the canonical axes $X, Y, Z$ by angles $\phi, \theta, \psi$ is defined by $3\times 3$ elementary rotation matrices:

### Rotation around X-axis (Roll $\phi$):
$$
R_x(\phi) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \cos\phi & -\sin\phi \\ 0 & \sin\phi & \cos\phi \end{bmatrix}
$$

### Rotation around Y-axis (Pitch $\theta$):
$$
R_y(\theta) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & 0 & \sin\theta \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -\sin\theta & 0 & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}
$$

### Rotation around Z-axis (Yaw $\psi$):
$$
R_z(\psi) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\psi & -\sin\psi & 0 \\ \sin\psi & \cos\psi & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

---

## 2. Non-Commutativity of 3D Rotations

Unlike numbers or 2D rotations, **3D rotations do not commute**:

$$
R_x(\phi) R_y(\theta) \neq R_y(\theta) R_x(\phi)
$$

The sequence of rotations dictates the final spatial orientation. In aerospace and robotics, the most common standard is the **Tait-Bryan ZYX convention** (Yaw $\to$ Pitch $\to$ Roll):

$$
R_{ZYX}(\psi, \theta, \phi) = R_z(\psi) R_y(\theta) R_x(\phi)
$$

Multiplying these matrices yields the complete $3\times 3$ rotation matrix in $SO(3)$:

$$
R_{ZYX} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\psi\cos\theta & \cos\psi\sin\theta\sin\phi - \sin\psi\cos\phi & \cos\psi\sin\theta\cos\phi + \sin\psi\sin\phi \\ \sin\psi\cos\theta & \sin\psi\sin\theta\sin\phi + \cos\psi\cos\phi & \sin\psi\sin\theta\cos\phi - \cos\psi\sin\phi \\ -\sin\theta & \cos\theta\sin\phi & \cos\theta\cos\phi \end{bmatrix}
$$

## 4. The Gimbal Lock Singularity

When Pitch reaches $\theta = \pm 90^\circ$ ($\pm \frac{\pi}{2}\text{ radians}$), $\cos\theta = 0$. In the matrix above:

$$
R_{ZYX}(\psi, 90^\circ, \phi) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \sin(\phi - \psi) & \cos(\phi - \psi) \\ 0 & \cos(\phi - \psi) & -\sin(\phi - \psi) \\ -1 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
$$

Notice that $\phi$ (Roll) and $\psi$ (Yaw) now appear only as the difference $(\phi - \psi)$. The system has **lost one degree of rotational freedom**. This condition is known as **Gimbal Lock**.

To eliminate singularities in flight controllers and 3D SLAM, robotics utilizes **Unit Quaternions**:

$$
\mathbf{q} = \begin{bmatrix} q_w \\ q_x \\ q_y \\ q_z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\theta/2) \\ \mathbf{u}\sin(\theta/2) \end{bmatrix}, \quad \|\mathbf{q}\| = 1
$$

---

## 5. Homogeneous Transformation in $SE(3)$

Combining 3D rotation $R \in SO(3)$ and translation $\mathbf{t} = [t_x, t_y, t_z]^T$ creates a $4\times 4$ homogeneous transformation matrix $T \in SE(3)$:

$$
T = \begin{bmatrix} R & \mathbf{t} \\ \mathbf{0}^T & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} r_{11} & r_{12} & r_{13} & t_x \\ r_{21} & r_{22} & r_{23} & t_y \\ r_{31} & r_{32} & r_{33} & t_z \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$
