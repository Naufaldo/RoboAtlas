# Coordinate Frames, SE(2) Transforms & Frame Trees (M04)

> "When a LiDAR mounted on a moving robot detects an obstacle at (x, y), what is the obstacle's true coordinate in the global factory floor map?"

In autonomous robotics, there is never just one coordinate frame. A complete robot system maintains a hierarchical tree of coordinate reference frames (such as ROS TF2):

```text
World Frame {W}
      ↓ (Robot Odometry Pose T_W^R)
Robot Body Frame {R}
      ↓ (Sensor Mounting Calibration T_R^S)
LiDAR Sensor Frame {S}
```

---

## 1. Homogeneous Coordinates & The Special Euclidean Group SE(2)

Pure rotation is linear ($R \mathbf{p}$), but translation is affine ($\mathbf{p} + \mathbf{t}$). To combine rotation and translation into a single unified matrix multiplication, we augment 2D coordinates into **homogeneous coordinates** $[x, y, 1]^T \in \mathbb{P}^2$.

The **Homogeneous Transformation Matrix $T \in \text{SE}(2)$** is:

$$
T = \begin{bmatrix} R(\theta) & \mathbf{t} \\ \mathbf{0}^T & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta & t_x \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta & t_y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

Transforming a point $P^B$ defined in frame $\{B\}$ to frame $\{A\}$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} x^A \\ y^A \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta & t_x \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta & t_y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x^B \\ y^B \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

---

## 2. Derivation: Frame Chain Composition & Analytical Inverse

### 2.1 Transform Chain Composition
To compute the transformation from World frame $\{W\}$ to Sensor frame $\{S\}$, we multiply along the transform tree:

$$
T_W^S = T_W^R \cdot T_R^S
$$

Given a detected laser beam reflection point $\mathbf{p}^S$ measured by the LiDAR, its global coordinate in the map $\mathbf{p}^W$ is:

$$
\mathbf{p}^W = T_W^S \cdot \mathbf{p}^S = T_W^R \cdot T_R^S \cdot \mathbf{p}^S
$$

*(Note: Matrix multiplication is non-commutative: $T_1 T_2 \neq T_2 T_1$. Order matters!)*

### 2.2 Analytical Matrix Inverse $T^{-1}$
Because of the structured block form, the exact analytical inverse of an SE(2) transformation is:

$$
T^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} R^T & -R^T \mathbf{t} \\ \mathbf{0}^T & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$
