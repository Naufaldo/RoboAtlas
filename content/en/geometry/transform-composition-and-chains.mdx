# Transform Composition, Matrix Non-Commutativity & Kinematic Chains (M05)

> "In what sequence should a robot multiply transformation matrices to calculate where an end-effector or onboard sensor is relative to the world map?"

When building kinematic chains for articulated robot arms or cascading transformations in autonomous vehicles (e.g. Map $\to$ Odom $\to$ Base Link $\to$ Camera), the order of matrix multiplication is fundamental.

---

## 1. Post-Multiplication vs. Pre-Multiplication Rules

In spatial geometry, matrix multiplication is **non-commutative**:

$$
T_1 T_2 \neq T_2 T_1
$$

### 1.1 Post-Multiplication (Local Frame Successive Transformations)
When every subsequent translation or rotation occurs **relative to the newly transformed local coordinate frame**:

$$
T_{\text{final}} = T_1 \cdot T_2 \cdot T_3 \cdots T_n
$$

*(We multiply on the right. This is standard in forward kinematics and Denavit-Hartenberg conventions).*

### 1.2 Pre-Multiplication (Fixed World Frame Transformations)
When transformations occur **relative to the static, unmoving global World frame $\{W\}$**:

$$
T_{\text{final}} = T_n \cdots T_3 \cdot T_2 \cdot T_1
$$

*(We multiply on the left).*

---

## 2. Derivation: Visualizing $T_{\text{trans}} T_{\text{rot}}$ vs. $T_{\text{rot}} T_{\text{trans}}$

Consider a pure translation matrix $T_{\text{trans}}(\mathbf{t})$ and a pure rotation matrix $T_{\text{rot}}(\theta)$:

### Order A: Translate First, Then Rotate ($T_A = T_{\text{trans}} \cdot T_{\text{rot}}$)
$$
T_A = \begin{bmatrix} I & \mathbf{t} \\ \mathbf{0}^T & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R(\theta) & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0}^T & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} R(\theta) & \mathbf{t} \\ \mathbf{0}^T & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$
The origin moves to $\mathbf{t}$, and the frame rotates in place at position $\mathbf{t}$.

### Order B: Rotate First, Then Translate in World ($T_B = T_{\text{rot}} \cdot T_{\text{trans}}$)
$$
T_B = \begin{bmatrix} R(\theta) & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0}^T & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} I & \mathbf{t} \\ \mathbf{0}^T & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} R(\theta) & R(\theta)\mathbf{t} \\ \mathbf{0}^T & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$
The origin position is **rotated** by $\theta$ to the new coordinate $R(\theta)\mathbf{t}$, ending up in a completely different location in space!
