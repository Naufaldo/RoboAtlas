# Configuration Space (C-Space) & Minkowski Sum Obstacle Inflation

> "How do we plan motion for a robot with complex geometric shape without checking thousands of geometric vertex collisions at every single step?"

Planning motion directly for an object with physical width and length in the physical **Workspace $\mathcal{W}$** is computationally expensive. The seminal breakthrough by Lozano-Pérez (1983) transforms the problem into **Configuration Space ($\mathcal{C}$-Space)**, where the robot is represented as an infinitesimal **point**.

---

## 1. The Minkowski Sum $\oplus$

To shrink the robot geometry $\mathcal{A}$ to a single point at origin $(0, 0)$, all physical obstacles $\mathcal{W}_{\text{obs}} \subset \mathcal{W}$ must be mathematically **inflated** by the reflected robot shape $-\mathcal{A}$:

The forbidden Configuration Space obstacle region $\mathcal{C}_{\text{obs}}$ is defined by the **Minkowski Sum**:

$$
\mathcal{C}_{\text{obs}} = \mathcal{W}_{\text{obs}} \oplus (-\mathcal{A}) = \left\{ w - a \mid w \in \mathcal{W}_{\text{obs}}, \, a \in \mathcal{A} \right\}
$$

Where:
- $\mathcal{W}_{\text{obs}}$: Physical obstacles in the 2D/3D workspace.
- $\mathcal{A}$: Robot footprint geometry.
- $\mathcal{C}_{\text{free}} = \mathcal{C} \setminus \mathcal{C}_{\text{obs}}$: Safe collision-free configuration space.

For a circular mobile robot of radius $r$, the Minkowski sum simply expands every obstacle boundary outward by Euclidean distance $r$, rounding all sharp corners into circular arcs.

---

## 2. The Point-Robot Planning Advantage

Once $\mathcal{C}_{\text{obs}}$ is computed, path planning algorithms (such as A*, Dijkstra, or RRT*) only need to verify if the robot center $(x, y) \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{free}}$. A single coordinate check replaces hundreds of polygon intersection tests!
